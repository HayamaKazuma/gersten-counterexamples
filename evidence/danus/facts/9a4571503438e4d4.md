## statement
Let P(y)=5+10y+10y^2+5y^3+y^4, F=P(y)+x^4+Txy^3, R_0=Z_(5)[T,x,y]/(F), m=(5,x,y)⊂R_0, A=(R_0)_m, and S=R_0\m. Fix a primitive fifth root of unity rho, put E=Q(rho), W=Z_(5)[rho], u=rho−1, B=A⊗_(Z_(5))W, and mathcal T=Spec B[1/u,1/y]. For g∈{1,2,3,4}, put a_g=rho^g−1. Set Qcal=Spec E[T,x,y,y^(-1)]/(F), D_g=V(x,y−a_g)⊂Qcal, and Qcal_s=D(s)⊂Qcal for each s∈S. Then Qcal is a smooth geometrically integral surface, mathcal T=Spec S^(-1)O(Qcal), and Pic(Qcal)=Z^4/Z(1,1,1,1), with standard basis mapping to O(D_g). This Picard calculation persists under every coefficient field extension of E, and Pic(Qcal)→Pic(mathcal T) is injective. For every fixed embedding E→C, the classes e_g=c_1^top(O(D_g)) in H^2(Qcal(C),R) have exactly the real diagonal relation. Moreover, for every s∈S, restriction H^2(Qcal(C),R)→H^2(Qcal_s(C),R) is injective. Here Pic is the group of line bundles and H^2 is singular cohomology.

## proof
We prove arithmetic Picard persistence first and then its Betti consequence. All localization sets in this proof are those explicitly defined in the statement.

1. Rings and the ramified local surface. In the UFD Z_(5)[x,y], the coefficients xy^3 and P(y)+x^4 have no common irreducible factor: the only factors of xy^3 are x,y, and neither divides P(y)+x^4. Thus F is a primitive linear polynomial in T and is irreducible over Q(x,y)[T]. Gauss's lemma makes R_0 a domain; its fraction field is Q(x,y), by solving for T. The ambient ring Z_(5)[T,x,y]_(5,x,y) is regular local of dimension three, maximal ideal (5,x,y), and residue field k_0=F_5(T). The image of F in the cotangent space is the nonzero class of 5, so A is regular local of dimension two. Since 5(1+2y+2y^2+y^3)=−(x^4+y^4+Txy^3), its maximal ideal is (x,y).

The polynomial P(u) is Eisenstein at 5. The ring W is finite free of rank four over Z_(5), is a domain, and its reduction modulo 5 is F_5[u]/(u^4), so it is local. The same displayed relation with x=0 says 5=u^4 epsilon with epsilon a unit of W. Consequently W is a one-dimensional regular local domain, hence a DVR with uniformizer u and residue field F_5. More precisely epsilon=5/u^4=−1/(1+2u+2u^2+u^3), whose reduction is −1.

B is finite free over A. Its closed fiber is k_0[u]/(u^4), so B has the single maximal ideal n=(u,x,y). Tensoring A↪Q(x,y) with the free Z_(5)-module W embeds B into Q(x,y)⊗_Q E=E(x,y); thus B is a domain. Put V=W[T]_(u). Every polynomial of W[T] whose reduction modulo u is nonzero becomes a unit in B, and comparison of localizations gives
 B=(V[x,y]/(F))_(u,x,y).
V is a DVR with residue field k_0 and uniformizer u. Inverting u and y in B gives exactly S^(-1)E[T,x,y,y^(-1)]/(F), since W[1/u]=E and B=S^(-1)(R_0⊗W). This proves the claimed description of mathcal T.

2. Resolution and intersections. Let Ycal=Bl_n(Spec B), and use homogeneous tangent coordinates (U:X:Y) corresponding to (u,x,y). The associated graded ring is
 k_0[U,X,Y]/(X^4+Y^4+TXY^3−U^4).
Indeed the ambient associated graded ring is polynomial, F has order four there, and multiplication by its nonzero initial form shows that the initial ideal of the principal ideal (F) is generated by that form. The exceptional scheme is therefore the plane curve C given by this homogeneous equation.

C is geometrically smooth. A geometric singular point has U=0 from the U derivative. It cannot also have Y=0, since the equation would then force X=0. Writing s=X/Y, the other equations force s^4+Ts+1=4s^3+T=0. In characteristic five the latter gives T=s^3 and the former gives s^4=2, whence T^4=3. This is impossible for the transcendental T. A smooth projective plane curve is geometrically integral in this case: distinct geometric components would intersect by Bezout and create a singular point; repeated components would also make it singular.

For completeness the strict-transform equations, with epsilon=5/u^4 retained as a coefficient in V, are
 u-chart: x=uX, y=uY,
 h_u=X^4+Y^4+TXY^3+epsilon(1+2uY+2u^2Y^2+u^3Y^3)=0;
 x-chart: u=xU, y=xY,
 h_x=1+Y^4+TY^3+epsilon U^4(1+2xY+2x^2Y^2+x^3Y^3)=0;
 y-chart: u=yU, x=yX,
 h_y=X^4+1+TX+epsilon U^4(1+2y+2y^2+y^3)=0.
The last two ambient chart rings retain u=xU or u=yU. The blowup charts of the regular local ambient ring are regular. Directly, in the x-chart one may eliminate x or U wherever the other is a unit, and at points with x=U=0 the relation u−xU has nonzero linear term u in a regular local polynomial ring over V. The y-chart has the same argument; the u-chart is a localization of V[X,Y]. In each chart the exceptional parameter is a nonzerodivisor, its quotient in the ambient chart is a polynomial domain over k_0, and the displayed h has nonzero reduction there. If exceptional_parameter·a=h·b, reduction modulo the exceptional parameter forces b divisible by that parameter, and cancellation then forces a divisible by h. Thus the quotient by h has no torsion for the exceptional parameter. These equations therefore describe the strict transform, without an extra component supported on the exceptional divisor.

At every point on C its geometric smoothness provides a nonzero differential of the reduced h in an exceptional-plane direction. Thus h is not in the square of the ambient maximal ideal, and the strict transform is regular there. Away from C it suffices to check B away from n. Where u is invertible, work in the smooth characteristic-zero hypersurface: if F_T=xy^3 vanishes, either x=0, where F_y=P'(y) is nonzero at the distinct roots of P, or y=0, where x^4=−5 and F_x=4x^3 is nonzero. At a nonclosed point over u=0, the special-fiber binary form x^4+y^4+Txy^3 is geometrically square-free by the same repeated-root argument, and its affine curve is smooth away from (x,y)=(0,0). Its x,y Jacobian proves regularity at these points. This proves Ycal regular everywhere and that the blowup is an isomorphism off n. It also proves B normal, since it is a hypersurface, hence Cohen–Macaulay, and is regular in codimension one.

The blowup exceptional ideal is n O_Ycal=O_Ycal(−C); restricting the degree-one tautological line of the Rees Proj gives O_Ycal(C)|_C=O_C(−1). This follows also from the transition functions of the displayed exceptional parameters. This is the general blowup identity of Stacks Project, Section 31.33, Lemmas 31.33.2 and 31.33.4, Tag 01OF: blowup charts are R[I/a], the extended ideal is locally generated by a, and O(−1)=O(exceptional divisor).

The prime (x,y−a_g) of B has strict transform Dtilde_g meeting C only at P_g=[1:0:g]. To see this, a_g/u=b_g is a unit with reduction g; the curve has parameter u and tangent direction [1:0:g]. On the u-chart, at X=0 the equation h_u is exactly product_(g=1)^4(Y−b_g), because P(uY)=u^4 product_g(Y−b_g). Its Y derivative is a unit at (u,X,Y−b_g). Consequently the maximal ideal there is generated by u and X, and Dtilde_g is locally X=0. Its intersection with C, locally u=0, has multiplicity one.

3. Exceptional Picard lattice. Let Cstar=C∩{UY≠0}. We claim that sum_g n_g P_g can be principal on Cstar, for integers n_g, only if all n_g are equal. Extend the constant field F_5 to k=algebraic closure of F_5 and consider the smooth affine surface
 M=Spec k[X,Z,Y,Y^(-1)]/(XZ−(1−Y^4)),
 with regular function T=(Z−X^3)/Y^3.
Smoothness follows since the derivative with respect to Y is 4Y^3, everywhere nonzero. On inverting X its coordinate ring is k[X^(±1),Y^(±1)], a UFD. The only prime divisors removed are L_g=V(X,Y−g), g=1,2,3,4, each with multiplicity one in div(X). Every divisor class on M is represented by their integral combination: any divisor becomes principal on the UFD open, and subtracting that principal divisor leaves a divisor supported on the L_g. A relation among them comes from a rational function whose restriction to this open has divisor zero, hence is a unit c X^r Y^s of its Laurent polynomial ring. Since Y is already a unit on M, its divisor is zero, whereas div(X)=sum_g L_g. Thus Pic(M)=Cl(M)=Z^4/Z(1,1,1,1).

For each t∈k the fiber T=t is the affine curve
 X^4+Y^4+tXY^3=1, Y≠0.
After setting s=X/Y and w=1/Y its ring is k[s,w,w^(-1)]/(w^4−(s^4+ts+1)). The polynomial s^4+ts+1 is not a square in k(s): a rational square that is polynomial is a polynomial square by unique factorization; after normalizing its leading coefficient, a square of degree four has form (s^2+bs+c)^2. Comparison of s^3 and s^2 coefficients gives b=c=0, contradicting constant term 1. Because k contains all fourth roots of unity and its characteristic is not two, w^4−q is irreducible when q is not a square. Explicitly, adjoining a root alpha gives a splitting field, with Galois group a subgroup of the fourth roots of unity. A degree one or two extension would make alpha^2 lie in k(s), forcing q to be a square. Thus the fiber is integral. The quotient defining the fiber is reduced and a domain; its single prime divisor occurs in div(T−t) with multiplicity one.

Localization to k(T) removes exactly these vertical prime divisors, since every closed point of A^1_k is some t∈k. Their classes are already zero, so divisor localization gives Pic(M)≅Pic(M_(k(T))). The generic fiber is Cstar_(k(T)), and L_g restricts to P_g with multiplicity one. Therefore a relation among the P_g after extension to k(T) is diagonal. Any relation over k_0 extends to this field and must likewise be diagonal.

4. Arithmetic Picard injection. Substitute v=x^3+Ty^3. Because y is inverted, this identifies Qcal with
 Spec E[x,v,y,y^(-1)]/(xv+P(y)).
It is smooth because P has four distinct roots a_g; at x=v=0 one has P'(y)≠0. Its open x≠0 is the Laurent UFD E[x^(±1),y^(±1)], and the removed divisors are exactly the four D_g, each with multiplicity one in div(x). Its units are c x^r y^s. The identical divisor argument given in step 3 yields Pic(Qcal)=Z^4/Z(diagonal). The argument also proves geometric integrality and is unchanged by any extension of E, since P's four distinct roots already belong to E.

Suppose sum_g n_g D_g becomes principal on mathcal T, say the divisor of a nonzero rational function f. On the regular surface Ycal, the divisor of f differs from sum_g n_g Dtilde_g by a divisor supported on Ycal\mathcal T. Here Ycal[1/u,1/y]=mathcal T: after u is inverted the blowup center is absent. All prime divisors in this complement are C or strict transforms of primes in u=0 or y=0 on Spec B. Write the difference as mC+sum_j m_j V_j. The strict transforms of u=0 meet C only in U=0, and those of y=0 meet it only in Y=0; this follows from their strict-transform equations in the blowup charts. Hence the V_j do not meet Cstar. The line O_C(−1) is trivial where U≠0. Restricting the principal-divisor line-bundle identity to Cstar therefore gives sum_g n_g[P_g]=0 in Pic(Cstar), using the transverse intersections already proved. Step 3 forces all n_g equal. The corresponding root divisor combination was already principal on Qcal, namely a multiple of div(x). This proves Pic(Qcal)→Pic(mathcal T) injective.

5. Betti classes and arbitrary permitted opens. Fix an embedding E→C. The D_g(C) are disjoint affine lines, and their complement in Qcal(C) is (C^×)^2 with coordinates x,y. The Thom isomorphism and the long exact cohomology sequence with supports give
 H^1((C^×)^2,R)→⊕_g R→H^2(Qcal(C),R).
The second map takes each standard basis vector to e_g. H^1 of the torus has basis the normalized angular classes of x and y. A small positive normal circle around each D_g has x winding once because x has simple zero, and y winding zero because y is a nonvanishing function there. Thus the first map has image precisely the diagonal line, which proves exactly the asserted relations among the e_g.

Fix s∈S and an irreducible E-divisor Z in the zero locus of s on Qcal. Its restriction to mathcal T is empty. The injective Picard restriction just proved implies [Z]=0 in Pic(Qcal). Over an algebraic closure Ebar of E the geometric components Z_1,...,Z_d of Z form one orbit under Gal(Ebar/E) and each has multiplicity one, because characteristic zero makes the generic field extension separable. The geometric Picard group is the torsion-free lattice already calculated; its generators D_g are E-defined, so this Galois action on the whole Picard group is trivial. Consequently every [Z_j] is the same element h, and base-changing [Z]=0 gives d h=0. Torsion-freeness implies h=0. Algebraically closed field extensions preserve geometric irreducibility here, so after extending Ebar to C the irreducible complex components still have zero Picard class and hence zero topological first Chern class.

Finally let H be the reduced complex divisor cut out by s. If H is empty the assertion is immediate. Otherwise remove the finite set Sigma of its singular points and component intersections. A point in a smooth real four-manifold has local cohomology with supports only in degree four, by the relative cohomology of a ball and its punctured ball. Thus H^2_Sigma(Qcal(C),R)=H^3_Sigma(Qcal(C),R)=0. The supports exact sequence identifies H^2_H(Qcal(C),R) with H^2_(H\Sigma)(Qcal(C)\Sigma,R). The latter is a direct sum of one R for each irreducible component, by the oriented real-rank-two Thom isomorphism; each smooth component remains connected. The image of each generator in ordinary degree-two cohomology is its divisor first Chern class, and all these classes were proved zero. Exactness of the restriction sequence
 H^2_H(Qcal(C),R)→H^2(Qcal(C),R)→H^2(Qcal_s(C),R)
therefore proves injectivity, for every s∈S.

## intuition
Arithmetic localization cannot kill the root divisor lattice, because its relation would restrict to the same nontrivial lattice on the exceptional quartic. Geometric Picard classes of all removed divisors then vanish, which prevents any loss of degree-two Betti cohomology.
