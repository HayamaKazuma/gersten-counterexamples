## statement
Put A_2=(Z_(5)[T,x,y]/(Phi_5(1+y)+x^4+Txy^3))_(5,x,y), where Z_(5) is the localization of the integers at (5), T,x,y are indeterminates, and Phi_5(z)=z^4+z^3+z^2+z+1. Let Ahat_2 be the (x,y)-adic completion of A_2. Both are regular local domains of dimension two, with maximal ideal (x,y) and residue field F_5(T). There is an actual class c in Quillen K_3(A_2) such that its image chat in K_3(Ahat_2) has infinite order, and c|_(Frac A_2)=0 and chat|_(Frac Ahat_2)=0 integrally. In particular both rational generic maps K_3(A_2)⊗Q→K_3(Frac A_2)⊗Q and K_3(Ahat_2)⊗Q→K_3(Frac Ahat_2)⊗Q have nonzero kernel.

## proof
We use precisely three accepted predecessors: 7aedefba09125cf6 for an actual arithmetic coefficient, 1e514828f5fb6c04 for its weighted completed quartic Chern detector, and 47409d3a276b6039 for the relative cyclic comparison and product. No comparison between actual K-groups and their spectral completions is assumed to be injective.

1. External K-theory statements and conventions. For a ring H, K(H) denotes its algebraic K-theory spectrum, K(H,I) the fiber of K(H)→K(H/I), and mathcalK(H)=K(H)^wedge_5[1/5]; use the same notation for a relative spectrum. The completion is the derived inverse limit of the spectra K(H)/5^n, rather than tensoring actual homotopy groups. Completion and inversion of 5 preserve fiber sequences in spectra.

We use the following published inputs with their full hypotheses relevant here. For regular noetherian rings of finite dimension, projective-module K-theory agrees with coherent-module G-theory. A finite closed immersion has a G-theory pushforward by restriction of scalars; flat base change commutes with it, and its projection formula is i_*(i^*alpha·z)=alpha·i_*z. If the closed immersion is an effective Cartier divisor with invertible ideal I, then i_*[O]=1−[I] in K_0. For a regular noetherian ring H and nonzero divisor s with H/s regular, localization and devissage give the fiber sequence K(H/s)→K(H)→K(H[1/s]), where the first arrow is pushforward, not reduction. These statements are Weibel, The K-book (2013), V.3.3, V.3.3.2, V.3.7.2, V.3.12, V.4.1 and V.6.1. In the uses of base change here, tensor product is exact because the ring map is flat; this also verifies the Tor-independence hypothesis of V.3.7.2. All schemes involved in that use are affine and their closed immersions are finite, so properness is satisfied. Weibel IV.6.4 states that K-groups commute with filtered colimits of rings; the same is true with finite coefficients, by the cofiber construction.

The second input is Geisser–Levine, The K-theory of fields in characteristic p, Inventiones 139 (2000), DOI 10.1007/s002229900034, Theorem 8.4: if X is a smooth variety of dimension d over a perfect field of characteristic p and n>=1, then K_j(X;Z/p^n)=0 for j>d. The group here is homotopy of K(X)/p^n, including its usual torsion contribution, rather than K_j(X) tensor Z/p^n. Its proof uses the motivic-to-K spectral sequence and the vanishing of logarithmic de Rham–Witt sheaves in weights greater than d. We apply exactly this smooth-variety statement in dimension two over a perfect field, followed by filtered localization. The original source, statement and proof have been read. Matlas searches for this precise vanishing returned related results but not this theorem, so the original source is the citation.

2. Regularity and the actual supported class. Abbreviate A=A_2 and Ahat=Ahat_2. In the regular local ring Z_(5)[T,x,y]_(5,x,y) of dimension three, with residue field F_5(T), the equation
 F=5+10y+10y^2+5y^3+y^4+x^4+Txy^3
has class 5 in the cotangent space. It belongs to the maximal ideal but not its square. Thus the quotient A is a regular local ring of dimension two. The identity
 5(1+2y+2y^2+y^3)=−(x^4+y^4+Txy^3)
shows that its maximal ideal is (x,y), since the parenthesis is a unit. A regular local ring is a domain. Completion preserves dimension and the cotangent space of a noetherian local ring; hence Ahat is also regular local of dimension two, with maximal ideal (x,y), residue field F_5(T), and is a domain. In particular x is a nonzero divisor in both rings.

Fix a primitive fifth root rho and put pi=rho−1 and W_ar=Z_(5)[rho]. Its defining shifted cyclotomic polynomial is Eisenstein at 5, so W_ar is a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer pi and residue F_5. The quotient
 A/xA = W_ar[T]_(pi)
uses y↦pi, where localization is at the prime generated by pi. It is a regular discrete valuation ring. Its completion is Ahat/xAhat, also regular. Let i:Spec(A/xA)→Spec A be the closed immersion.

Fact 7aedefba09125cf6 supplies an actual beta∈K_3(W_ar), integrally antisymmetric under sigma_(-1), whose reduction in K_3(F_5) is zero and whose AMMN point coordinate b∈E=Q_5(rho) has both odd character projections e_1b and e_3b nonzero. Here G=F_5^×, sigma_g(rho)=rho^g, and e_r=(1/4)sum_g omega_T(g)^(-r)sigma_g, with omega_T the Teichmuller character and r=1,3. Define
 c=i_*(beta|_(A/xA))∈K_3(A).
This is an actual integral K-class, using the regular-ring K=G equivalence. Its restriction to A[1/x] is zero: every coherent module in the pushforward is killed by x and becomes zero after that localization. Therefore c restricts to zero over Frac A. The completion map A→Ahat is flat. Flat base change identifies chat with the pushforward of beta to the Cartier divisor x=0 in Spec Ahat. The same support argument gives chat|_(Frac Ahat)=0 integrally.

3. The test algebra and its essentially smooth model. Set k=overline(F_5), W=W(k), R=W[rho] and V=lim_n W[T]_(5)/5^n. By fact 1e514828f5fb6c04 choose the finite unramified extension C/V obtained by generic-special completion of a smooth affine W-curve D etale over an open of the parameter line. Its connected special fiber has function field L, a finite separable extension of k(T). Put C'=C⊗_W R and
 B=C'〈X,Y〉/(X^4+Phi_5(1+pi Y)/pi^4+TXY^3).
The coefficient polynomial is integral over R: the valuations of the coefficients below degree four in Phi_5(1+pi Y) are at least four in the pi-adic normalization. The ring C' is a complete discrete valuation ring with uniformizer pi and residue L. Angle brackets mean restricted power series, or equivalently the pi-adic completion of the polynomial algebra with the indicated quotient. The top coefficient in Y is one, so the equation is monic in Y; its quotient and its completion are pi-torsion-free.

The special fiber is
 B/pi B = L[X,Y]/(X^4+Y^4+TXY^3−1).
It is smooth over L. Indeed for the degree-four homogeneous polynomial G_0=X^4+Y^4+TXY^3, Euler's identity is X(G_0)_X+Y(G_0)_Y=4G_0, which is the unit 4 on G_0=1. Its projective closure is the smooth quartic over the generic parameter used in fact 1e514828f5fb6c04, so it is geometrically integral. In particular the special fiber is a domain. The monic presentation over the noetherian C' and adic completion show that B is noetherian. Since pi is in its Jacobson radical, every maximal ideal contains pi. At such a maximal ideal its quotient by pi is regular and pi is a nonzero divisor, so lifting a regular system of parameters of that quotient and adjoining pi gives a regular local ring. Thus B is regular at all maximal ideals and therefore regular everywhere. Its dimension is two: the restricted two-variable polynomial ring over C' has dimension three and we quotient by a nonzero monic equation. Alternatively the same local parameter argument gives local dimension at most two, with equality at closed points of the special curve. Adic separatedness, pi-torsion-freeness and the domain special fiber show that B is a domain by taking the first nonzero pi-adic terms of any two nonzero elements.

Here is the finite-type smooth model needed for fact 47409d3a276b6039. Let U be the localization of D at its generic special point, so C is its 5-adic completion. Before completion consider
 H=((U⊗_W R)[X,Y])/(X^4+Phi_5(1+pi Y)/pi^4+TXY^3).
Its pi-adic completion is B. The special fiber of H is smooth over L by the Euler identity. The two relative partial derivatives therefore generate the unit ideal modulo pi. Choose j in their generated ideal with j≡1 modulo pi. Localizing H at j makes the hypersurface smooth of relative dimension one over U⊗_W R. Since j≡1 modulo pi, this localization does not change the completion. The ring U⊗_W R is a localization of the smooth R-algebra D⊗_W R, of relative dimension one. By transitivity of smoothness, H[1/j] is a localization of a smooth R-algebra of relative dimension two with finite projective cotangent module of rank two. Every finite choice of coefficients or j can be spread over a further localization of D. This constructs the precise B_0 required in fact 47409d3a276b6039; its 5-adic and pi-adic completions agree because 5 is a unit times pi^4.

For each g∈G put a_g=(rho^g−1)/pi and J_g=(X,Y−a_g)⊂B. Fact 1e514828f5fb6c04 proves these ideals are invertible and
 Xi=sum_(g∈G) sigma_g(b)(−c_1^dR(J_g))
is nonzero in Omega^2_(B/R)[1/5]/d Omega^1_(B/R)[1/5].
The differential modules are continuous relative to R and include the parameter differential dT. The differential image here is the ordinary image, without taking closure.

4. Detection of an actual class on B. Write W_E=Z_5[rho]. The image of beta in K_3(W_E) has zero reduction in K_3(F_5). Hence it lifts to an actual class beta_rel in K_3(W_E,(pi)). The lift is unique because K_4(F_5)=0, by the finite-field calculation already recorded and used in fact 7aedefba09125cf6. The naturality under sigma_g makes the lifts of all conjugates compatible. Pull them to K_3(R,(pi)) through W_E→R. Their relative point coordinates are precisely sigma_g(b) viewed in R[1/5]: the AMMN equivalences defining the point coordinate are natural under this extension of coefficient points, and the induced map on degree-two cyclic homology of a point is the inclusion of the coefficient fields.

Form the actual relative class
 delta_rel=sum_(g∈G) (sigma_g beta_rel)|_B · (1−[J_g]) ∈K_3(B,pi B),
using the K(B)-module action on the fiber of reduction. Denote its image in actual K_3(B) by delta. Applying fact 47409d3a276b6039 to the model in step 3, the top-form component of delta_rel after rationalized spectral completion is exactly Xi. It is nonzero by the preceding step. Consequently the image of delta_rel in pi_3 mathcalK(B,pi B) is nonzero.

We next justify two separate injections. The ring B/pi B is the filtered colimit of the coordinate rings
 D_0[1/s][X,Y]/(X^4+Y^4+TXY^3−1),
where D_0=D/5D and s ranges through its nonzero elements. These are smooth affine varieties of dimension two over the perfect field k: D_0[1/s] is a smooth affine curve, and the Euler identity gives a smooth relative affine curve over it. Geisser–Levine Theorem 8.4 and filtered-colimit continuity therefore give
 K_j(B/pi B;Z/5^n)=0 for every j>2 and n>=1.
For every j>=3 the homotopy groups in degrees j and j+1 of the entire finite-coefficient tower vanish. The Milnor exact sequence for its inverse limit then gives pi_j K(B/pi B)^wedge_5=0. Inverting 5 preserves this vanishing. In particular pi_3 mathcalK(B/pi B)=pi_4 mathcalK(B/pi B)=0.

First, the reduction fiber sequence
 mathcalK(B,pi B)→mathcalK(B)→mathcalK(B/pi B)
shows that pi_3 mathcalK(B,pi B)→pi_3 mathcalK(B) is an isomorphism. Thus the image of delta in pi_3 mathcalK(B) is nonzero.

Second, regularity of B and its quotient, together with pi being a nonzero divisor, permits localization and devissage:
 mathcalK(B/pi B)→mathcalK(B)→mathcalK(B[1/pi]).
Here the left arrow is the support pushforward. Since its source has zero pi_3, the map pi_3 mathcalK(B)→pi_3 mathcalK(B[1/pi]) is injective. Therefore delta|_(B[1/pi]) has nonzero image in pi_3 mathcalK(B[1/pi]). This target is a Q_5-vector space, so delta|_(B[1/pi]) is an actual element of infinite order. This conclusion uses only the implication that a torsion actual class has zero image in a characteristic-zero vector space; it requires no injection of actual K-theory into its completion.

5. Matching the original Gysin class after inverting pi. There is a ring map A→B given by x↦pi X, y↦pi Y and by the named parameter T. The defining equation maps to pi^4 times the defining equation of B, hence to zero. An element inverted when defining A has reduction outside (5,x,y), hence a nonzero polynomial in T modulo that ideal. Its image modulo pi is a nonzero element of L and is a unit of B/pi B. Because pi lies in the Jacobson radical, its image in B is a unit. This verifies the localization map. Its image of (x,y) is contained in pi B, so pi-adic completeness of B gives a continuous extension Ahat→B. Both maps induce compatible maps to B[1/pi].

To compute the actual class, first make a flat coefficient extension where the supported divisor splits. Set
 S=(A⊗_(Z_(5))W_ar)[1/5].
This is flat over A. It is regular: it is finite etale over A[1/5], because Q(rho)/Q is separable. In S the polynomial Phi_5(1+y) splits with distinct roots rho^g−1 whose differences are units. Thus the divisor x=0 is the disjoint union of the four Cartier divisors with ideals
 I_g=(x,y−(rho^g−1))⊂S.
To verify the Cartier assertion, near this component the other polynomial factors are units, and the equation F=0 expresses y−(rho^g−1) as a multiple of x; elsewhere that ideal is the unit ideal. The element x remains a nonzero divisor under the flat extension. The component rings are localizations of Q(rho)[T], so they are regular.

On the g-th component the original coefficient map W_ar→A/xA sends rho=1+y to rho^g. The flat-base-change and projection-formula statements in step 1 therefore give the equality of actual classes
 c|_S = sum_(g∈G) (sigma_g beta)|_S · (1−[I_g]) ∈K_3(S).
No scalar projector is applied to any actual class in this formula.

The map A→B and the coefficient embedding W_ar→C' give S→B[1/pi]. Under it x=pi X and y−(rho^g−1)=pi(Y−a_g). Hence I_g pulls back as an invertible module to J_g[1/pi]. More explicitly, the Cartier ideal map to the displayed generated ideal is an isomorphism: locally I_g is generated by x on its support, whose image pi X is a nonzero divisor in the domain B[1/pi], and away from that support it is generated by a unit. Consequently pulling back the actual equality on S gives
 c|_(B[1/pi]) = sum_g (sigma_g beta)|_(B[1/pi])·(1−[J_g[1/pi]])
              =delta|_(B[1/pi]).
The right side has infinite order by step 4. Since the map from A to B[1/pi] factors through Ahat, it is also the image of chat. Thus chat has infinite order; this also implies c has infinite order.

The integral generic vanishings were proved in step 2. An element of an abelian group maps to zero after tensoring with Q exactly when it is torsion. Therefore c and chat give nonzero elements of the two rational generic kernels asserted in the statement. This proves the complete central theorem.

## intuition
The weighted continuous Chern class detects an actual relative K3 product; two distinct special-fiber vanishings preserve it through reduction and generic localization. Flat supported base change matches it with one Gysin class already defined before completion.
