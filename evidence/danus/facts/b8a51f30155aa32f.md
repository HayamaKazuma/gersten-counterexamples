## statement
Let p be a prime, W_0=W(overline{F}_p), and W_ram a finite totally ramified extension of the complete discrete valuation ring W_0, with uniformizer pi and fraction field E_ram=W_ram[1/p]. Let B_alg=S^{-1}A_sm, where A_sm is a smooth commutative W_ram-algebra and S is an arbitrary multiplicative subset. Assume Omega^1_{B_alg/W_ram} is finite projective of constant rank d_rel, a finite nonnegative integer. Put B_cmp=lim_r B_alg/p^r B_alg and define Omega_cmp^j=lim_r(Omega^j_{B_alg/W_ram}/p^r), placed in homological degree j; set Omega_cmp^j=0 for j outside [0,d_rel]. The de Rham differential extends continuously to these modules.

Write K(B_cmp,pi B_cmp)=fib(K(B_cmp)->K(B_cmp/pi B_cmp)) for actual relative algebraic K-theory. A superscript wedge_p means derived spectrum p-completion; [1/p] means inverting p after that completion, not tensoring actual K-groups with Q_p. For F=HH,HC,HC^-,HP define F^cts(B_cmp/W_ram;Q_p)=(Rlim_r F((B_cmp/p^r)/W_ram))^wedge_p[1/p], with Hochschild and cyclic theories derived over W_ram.

There is a natural character
chi_cmp: K(B_cmp,pi B_cmp) -> Sigma HC^cts(B_cmp/W_ram;Q_p)
whose factorization through the canonical actual-to-completed-and-rationalized map has second arrow an equivalence:
K(B_cmp,pi B_cmp)^wedge_p[1/p] ≃ Sigma HC^cts(B_cmp/W_ram;Q_p).
This equivalence and chi_cmp are K(B_cmp)-linear, where the target action is restricted along the canonical negative-cyclic character K(B_cmp)->HC^{-,cts}(B_cmp/W_ram;Q_p).

The target is calculated by the finite mixed complex (Omega_cmp^*,0,d)[1/p]. Explicitly, if h has homological degree -2, its cyclic complex is
CC^+(Omega_cmp)=((W_ram[h,h^{-1}]/hW_ram[h]) tensor_{W_ram} Omega_cmp^*, h d),
and HC^cts(B_cmp/W_ram;Q_p) ≃ CC^+(Omega_cmp)[1/p].
The corresponding negative and periodic total complexes of this same mixed complex compute HC^{-,cts} and HP^cts. Every total degree has only finitely many form summands. All homology is ordinary algebraic homology; differential images are not replaced by their topological closures. These conclusions allow arbitrary S and arbitrary p relative to d_rel.

## proof
The proof uses no project fact as an implicit generalization. All auxiliary assertions are proved here or are the explicitly scoped external results below. The statement is the essentially smooth strengthening interface only.

1. External inputs, definitions, and the maps being used.

(1a) Antieau--Mathew--Morrow--Nikolaus, On the Beilinson fiber square, arXiv:2003.12541v2 (2021), Theorem A: for a commutative ordinary ring T henselian along (p), their natural square
K(T)^wedge_p[1/p] -> K(T/p)^wedge_p[1/p]
down                         down
HC^-(T/Z)^wedge_p[1/p] -> HP(T/Z)^wedge_p[1/p]
is cartesian. The lower left map is the canonical negative cyclic character. Consequently its horizontal fiber identifies K(T,(p))^wedge_p[1/p] with Sigma HC(T/Z)^wedge_p[1/p]. Their notation ;Q_p has precisely this completion-then-inversion meaning. Henselian is a condition on the pair (T,(p)), not finite presentation.

AMMN Corollary 3.8 states that if p is nilpotent in an associative ring T and I is a nilpotent ideal, relative K(T,I) and relative TC(T,I) are quasi-isogenous to zero. Here this means every bounded Postnikov truncation is killed by some integer; in the p-primary situation a p-power suffices. Thus they vanish after p-completion and inverting p. Its proof applies Theorem 3.4 to the nilpotent quotient: the quotient map is already a quasi-isogeny of underlying rings when p is nilpotent.

AMMN Definition 4.1 defines continuous theories by homotopy inverse limit of the ordinary p-power thickenings. Proposition 4.2 states that for a qcqs scheme with bounded p-power torsion the maps from its HH, THH, HC^-, HP, and TC to the continuous invariants of its formal p-completion are p-adic equivalences. The paragraph immediately preceding Proposition 4.10 explicitly includes the corresponding theories relative to the base integer ring. No finite-presentation assumption occurs in Proposition 4.2. HC follows as well from the norm fiber sequence, or from the connective circle-orbit construction. The proof reduces to affine rings, uses the cyclic bar construction for THH, base change for HH, and then the circle constructions. We use Proposition 4.2 separately for B_alg and B_cmp, whose p-power reductions coincide.

We do not apply the smooth-formal-scheme hypothesis of Proposition 4.10 to an arbitrary completed localization. Instead we reconstruct its essential absolute-to-relative comparison below.

(1b) The structural HKR facts used are the following, over any commutative discrete base k. Derived HH(T/k) for a connective simplicial commutative k-algebra T admits its natural circle-equivariant descending HKR filtration. The ith associated graded is (L exterior^i L_{T/k})[i], and the ith filtration term is i-connective in homological conventions. In particular the filtration is complete. For a smooth discrete k-algebra the cotangent complex is Omega^1 in degree zero and the antisymmetrization map Omega^j -> HH_j is an isomorphism; the graded algebra multiplication corresponds to exterior multiplication.

These statements, with the arbitrary-base convention, are given and constructed in Benjamin Antieau, Periodic cyclic homology and derived de Rham cohomology, arXiv:1808.05246, Sections 3 and 4 (2019). Section 4 obtains the filtration by extending the polynomial algebra construction over simplicial polynomial resolutions; connectivity proves completeness. This construction is compatible with change of base and localization, and the associated graded maps are the derived exterior powers of the cotangent maps. One can also see localization compatibility directly from cotangent base change and the bar construction. We only use the underlying HH filtration, not an infinite rational splitting of periodic cyclic homology.

For completeness, in the smooth affine case the antisymmetrization assertion can be checked on an etale coordinate chart using the Koszul resolution of the diagonal: the generators x_i tensor 1-1 tensor x_i form the regular sequence of the polynomial diagonal, and after tensoring to the diagonal its Koszul differential is zero. The comparison to the bar resolution sends a wedge of generators to the signed sum of the corresponding bars. Etale base change gives the same computation on smooth charts; localization and the faithfully flat local criterion identify the global map. Thus this version does not assert that the raw map a_0[a_1|...|a_j] -> a_0 da_1 wedge ... wedge da_j is itself an integral isomorphism; the factorial distinction is retained below.

(1c) Nikolaus--Scholze, On topological cyclic homology, arXiv:1707.01799, Section I.3 and Theorem I.4.1/Corollary I.4.3, provide the natural lax monoidal fixed-point and Tate functors and the circle norm sequence
Sigma X_{hS^1} -> X^{hS^1} -> X^{tS^1}.
For a circle-equivariant algebra and its module, this is a sequence of modules over the homotopy fixed points of that algebra. This compatibility can also be obtained by constructing the norm within the module category and then forgetting modules. Bökstedt--Ottosen, A Hochschild--Kostant--Rosenberg theorem for cyclic homology, arXiv:1601.07412v2, Section 2 (Definition 2.1, Remark 2.2, Proposition 2.3), give the mixed-complex cyclic, negative and periodic totalizations and their negative-cyclic module action over a general commutative base. These statements precede their special characteristic-two results. Hoyois, The homotopy fixed points of the circle action on Hochschild homology, arXiv:1506.07123v2, Theorems 2.1 and 2.3, identify this mixed-complex description with the circle constructions; in particular the cyclic totalization computes homotopy orbits.

(1d) Blumberg--Gepner--Tabuada, Uniqueness of the multiplicative cyclotomic trace, arXiv:1103.3923, Theorems 7.3 and 7.4, construct the multiplicative Dennis and cyclotomic traces. In particular for a commutative ring T the trace K(T)->TC(T) is multiplicative and makes relative trace maps K(T)-linear. The TC comparison used in (1a) is the one actually constructed in AMMN Proposition 2.8, Corollary 2.10, Theorem 2.12, and Definition 2.14. We track its module structure explicitly in Step 6; naturality alone is not used as a substitute for module compatibility.

2. Ring-theoretic preparation and the completed cotangent complex.

A smooth W_ram-algebra is finitely presented and W_ram-flat; its arbitrary localization B_alg is still noetherian and W_ram-flat. Its cotangent complex relative to W_ram is its finite projective differential module in degree zero, since localization has zero relative cotangent complex. Finite projective exterior powers vanish beyond d_rel.

The ideal-adic completion of a noetherian ring is flat over that ring, and completion of a finite projective module is its tensor product with the completed ring (this follows by expressing it as the image of a finite idempotent matrix). Thus B_cmp is W_ram-flat and Omega_cmp^j is a finite projective B_cmp-module. Both rings have zero p-power torsion. If B_cmp is the zero ring, all spectra and complexes in the statement vanish and the result holds; hence assume it is not zero. Since B_cmp is p-adically complete, (B_cmp,(p)) is henselian: the usual successive Newton lifting for a simple root converges, and 1-pb has inverse sum_{n>=0}(pb)^n for every b. Completeness for (p) and (pi) agrees because p is a unit times pi^e for the finite ramification index e.

Write c_diff=f'(pi), where W_ram=W_0[pi] and f is the monic Eisenstein polynomial of the uniformizer. The monogenic presentation holds for a finite totally ramified extension of complete discrete valuation rings. Its derivative is nonzero, because the fraction fields have characteristic zero; choose a nonnegative integer a_diff with p^{a_diff} in c_diff W_ram.

First, the derived p-completion of L_{W_0/Z} is zero. For each r>=1, W_0/p^r=W_r(overline{F}_p) is the filtered union over finite subfields of W_r(F_{p^m}), each finite etale over Z/p^r. Cotangent complexes commute with filtered colimits and vanish for etale maps. Since W_0 is Z-flat, derived base change identifies
L_{W_0/Z} tensor^L_{W_0} W_0/p^r = L_{(W_0/p^r)/(Z/p^r)}=0.
Taking the inverse limit proves the asserted completed vanishing.

Transitivity for Z -> W_0 -> W_ram now gives
(L_{W_ram/Z})^wedge_p = [W_ram --c_diff--> W_ram]
in cohomological degrees -1,0. Base change and transitivity for W_ram -> B_alg give a triangle over B_cmp
M_diff -> L_abs -> P_cmp,
where M_diff=[B_cmp --c_diff--> B_cmp], L_abs=(L_{B_alg/Z})^wedge_p, and P_cmp=Omega_cmp^1 in degree zero. Completion and these base changes commute here: the relevant modules are flat, the completed base cotangent is the displayed finite perfect complex, and the relative cotangent is finite projective. Alternatively reduce the entire transitivity triangle modulo p^r and pass to its derived limit.

The connecting map P_cmp->M_diff[1] is zero. Indeed finite projectivity of P_cmp computes its derived Hom into a complex termwise, and H^1(Hom(P_cmp,M_diff))=0 because M_diff has no positive cohomology. Consequently the projection L_abs->P_cmp admits a section and L_abs is (noncanonically) M_diff direct-sum P_cmp, with that projection. No natural splitting is needed.

Choose g_diff in W_ram with c_diff*g_diff=p^{a_diff}. The map multiplication by g_diff from degree zero to degree -1 is a chain homotopy between p^{a_diff} id_{M_diff} and zero. Thus this is annihilation as an object of the derived category, not just annihilation of cohomology groups.

For i>=1 the error in the ith derived exterior power of L_abs->P_cmp is the direct sum of terms
(L exterior^r M_diff) tensor exterior^{i-r} P_cmp, 1<=r<=i.
The derived exterior-power functor is homogeneous of degree r on scalar endomorphisms: its value on t id is t^r id. It is a functor on the derived infinity category of connective complexes, so it takes the homotopy p^{a_diff} id_{M_diff}~0 to a homotopy p^{r a_diff} id~0 on its rth exterior power. Here exterior^r of a zero map is a zero map for r>0. Hence each displayed summand is killed as an object by p^{r a_diff}. In particular the error for fixed i is killed by p^{i a_diff}.

This exterior-power calculation really computes the completed HKR graded pieces. For a fixed i, derived exterior powers commute with derived base change modulo p^r. The completed complex L_abs is perfect. Its ith derived exterior power is therefore perfect and derived p-complete. Reducing modulo p^r identifies it with the corresponding reduction of L exterior^i L_{B_alg/Z}. Passing to the limit identifies (L exterior^i L_{B_alg/Z})^wedge_p with L exterior^i L_abs. The same applies to P_cmp. There is no assertion that completion commutes with an arbitrary infinite localization colimit.

3. From absolute HH to relative HC: the finite-range and equivariant arguments.

Derived p-completion preserves c-connectivity for every integer c. To see this for a c-connective complex X, each X/p^r is c-connective. The only possible extra group of Rlim_r X/p^r below c would be in degree c-1 and is lim^1_r pi_c(X/p^r). But pi_c(X/p^r)=pi_c(X)/p^r, with surjective transition maps, so that lim^1 is zero. Degrees below c-1 vanish as well.

Apply the completed HKR filtrations to
F_cmp: HH(B_alg/Z)^wedge_p -> HH(B_alg/W_ram)^wedge_p.
Their ith tails remain i-connective by the preceding paragraph and (1b). In any specified finite homotopy range, only finitely many graded pieces can contribute: truncate the filtration at an index strictly above that range. By Step 2 the cone of the map on each of those finitely many graded pieces is killed by a power of p as an underlying complex. A finite extension of objects killed by powers of p is killed by the product of those powers. Explicitly, in a triangle U->V->T, if p^a U=0 and p^b T=0, then p^b id_V factors through U by the exact sequence of morphisms; multiplying by p^a proves p^{a+b} id_V=0.

It follows that the connective cone C_abs of F_cmp has bounded p-power torsion in each fixed homotopy degree, with a bound on any fixed finite range. One may use one more filtration stage than the requested range to account for the long exact sequence of the cone. This gives finite-range quasi-isogeny, with no bound asserted uniform over all degrees.

It remains to justify taking circle orbits followed by completion. Regard C_abs in Fun(BS^1,D(Z_p)), with its pointwise homological t-structure. A heart object is an ordinary module with trivial circle action: the automorphism space of a degree-zero derived module is discrete (its positive homotopy groups would be negative Ext groups), while BS^1 is simply connected. For any N>=0, the object tau_{<=N} C_abs has a finite equivariant Postnikov tower, and each layer is a shifted module killed by a p-power. Applying the extension argument in this equivariant stable category gives an actual equivariant null-homotopy p^{b_N} id_{tau_{<=N} C_abs}~0 for some b_N. We have not promoted an underlying null-homotopy to an equivariant one without this argument.

Circle homotopy orbits and derived completion preserve such scalar null-homotopies. Moreover tau_{>=N+1} C_abs remains (N+1)-connective after homotopy orbits (a colimit of connective objects) and after completion by the connectivity argument just given. The triangle of the two truncations consequently shows that each degree <=N of ((C_abs)_{hS^1})^wedge_p is killed by p^{b_N}. Inverting p kills all these homotopy groups, so
((HH(B_alg/Z)^wedge_p)_{hS^1})^wedge_p[1/p]
 -> ((HH(B_alg/W_ram)^wedge_p)_{hS^1})^wedge_p[1/p]
is an equivalence.

These are the p-completed HC objects. Indeed for a connective circle complex, completion can be interchanged with the orbit construction followed by completion: in any fixed homotopy range a finite simplicial bar skeleton suffices. Each finite skeleton uses finite colimits of (S^1)^j_+ tensor X; these commute with derived completion because each (S^1)^j is a finite CW complex. The remaining skeletal tail becomes arbitrarily connective, and remains so on completion. Equivalently the comparison is a mod-p equivalence followed by completion.

Thus HC(B_alg/Z)^wedge_p[1/p] -> HC(B_alg/W_ram)^wedge_p[1/p] is an equivalence. Using (1a), Proposition 4.2 and its relative variant for B_alg and B_cmp, yields the same equivalence for B_cmp and identifies the relative term with HC^cts(B_cmp/W_ram;Q_p). Both rings satisfy the needed torsion and qcqs hypotheses, irrespective of S.

The norm sequences now give a cartesian square
HC^-(B_cmp/Z)^wedge_p[1/p] -> HP(B_cmp/Z)^wedge_p[1/p]
down                                       down
HC^{-,cts}(B_cmp/W_ram;Q_p) -> HP^cts(B_cmp/W_ram;Q_p),
because the induced map of its horizontal fibers is the equivalence just proved, suspended once. The two vertical maps need not be proved individually equivalent, and we make no such claim.

4. Finite integral mixed-HKR comparison.

Let C_bar be the normalized Hochschild mixed complex of the W_ram-flat algebra B_alg over W_ram. Its Hochschild differential is b, and its Connes operator is denoted B_Connes to distinguish it from ring notation. The target mixed complex is (Omega^*_{B_alg/W_ram},0,d), with forms in degrees 0 through d_rel.

For 0<=j<=d_rel define the integral map
q_HKR,j(a_0[a_1|...|a_j])=(d_rel!/j!) a_0 da_1 wedge ... wedge da_j,
and put q_HKR,j=0 for j>d_rel. All coefficients are integers. Commutativity and the Leibniz rule give q_HKR,j-1 b=0: adjacent product terms cancel with the two endpoint terms after expanding their differentials.

The normalized Connes formula in (1c) has j+1 cyclic summands. Their images under a_0[a_1|...|a_{j+1}] -> a_0 da_1 wedge ... wedge da_{j+1} coincide after the cyclic permutation signs are combined with the exterior signs. Hence for j<d_rel,
q_HKR,j+1 B_Connes=(j+1)d_rel!/(j+1)! times da_0 wedge ... wedge da_j
=d q_HKR,j.
For j=d_rel both sides are zero because Omega^{d_rel+1}=0. For larger degrees they are zero by definition. Thus q_HKR is a genuine integral map of mixed complexes, hence of circle objects.

Under the antisymmetrization isomorphism Omega^j -> HH_j from (1b), each signed permutation contributes the same wedge, so the composite with q_HKR,j is j!*(d_rel!/j!)=d_rel! times the identity. HH_j vanishes beyond d_rel. This holds for B_alg as follows: the assertion holds for the smooth algebra A_sm and is preserved under arbitrary localization. One can express the localization as the filtered colimit of its finite localizations; HH, forms, and the antisymmetrization maps commute with those colimits. Completion is not used in that localization argument.

Every form module is W_ram-flat. Therefore multiplication by d_rel! is injective, including when p divides d_rel!. The long exact sequence of the cone C_HKR shows
pi_j C_HKR=Omega^j_{B_alg/W_ram}/d_rel! for 0<=j<=d_rel,
and all other homotopy groups vanish. In particular there is no kernel contribution in degree d_rel+1. This addresses the cone amplitude before any equivariant construction.

Set v_fact=v_p(d_rel!). All primes different from p are units in W_ram, so every displayed quotient is killed by p^{v_fact}. The equivariant Postnikov tower of C_HKR has at most d_rel+1 layers; as in Step 3, its heart objects have trivial circle action. The extension argument proves
p^{(d_rel+1)v_fact} id_{C_HKR}~0
in Fun(BS^1,D(W_ram)).
This is a uniform bound on the entire cone. Consequently applying any of derived completion, circle homotopy orbits, homotopy fixed points, or the Tate functor retains a cone with the same scalar null-homotopy. After inverting p all of these induced maps are equivalences. Dividing q_HKR by d_rel! after rationalization gives the normalized formula 1/j!, without exchanging rationalization with any infinite product. If p>d_rel, q_HKR/d_rel! is already an integral mixed-complex equivalence; no such inequality is needed for the rational statement.

For the finite forms mixed complex, the cyclic totalization is precisely CC^+ in the statement. Negative and periodic totalizations use respectively nonnegative and all integer powers of h, with the usual product totalization. For any fixed total degree n, a contributing summand h^r Omega^j satisfies j-2r=n with 0<=j<=d_rel. There are only finitely many such j. This also proves degreewise finiteness for negative and periodic totalizations.

All these total complexes have W_ram-flat terms. Their derived tensor products with W_ram/p^s are their ordinary termwise reductions: the two-term free resolution [W_ram --p^s--> W_ram] and termwise injectivity of p^s prove this without a boundedness assumption on the total complex. The reduction towers are surjective in every degree. Their homotopy inverse limit is consequently their ordinary degreewise inverse limit. Finite sums in each degree show that this limit is exactly the total complex of (Omega_cmp^*,0,d). Proposition 4.2 in (1a) identifies the original completed invariants with the continuous invariants. The uniform cone bound therefore proves all the mixed-HKR statements.

Taking homology after inversion of p uses ordinary kernels and ordinary images in modules. The preceding calculation completed complexes and their terms, not a quotient after taking homology; it has no step replacing d Omega_cmp^{j-1} by its closure. This proves the ordinary-image assertion even if that image is not closed.

5. Constructing the equivalence from completed relative K-theory.

Apply (1a), Theorem A, to B_cmp, which is commutative and henselian along (p) by Step 2. Its square identifies
K(B_cmp,p B_cmp)^wedge_p[1/p]
with Sigma HC(B_cmp/Z)^wedge_p[1/p].
Step 3 identifies the latter with Sigma HC^cts(B_cmp/W_ram;Q_p).

The map B_cmp/p -> B_cmp/pi has nilpotent kernel: p is a unit times pi^e, so the kernel (pi)/(p) has e-th power zero. These rings are killed by p. AMMN Corollary 3.8 applies and shows that K(B_cmp/p)->K(B_cmp/pi) is an equivalence after p-completion and inversion of p; the same holds for TC. Passing to fibers gives a natural equivalence
K(B_cmp,p B_cmp)^wedge_p[1/p] -> K(B_cmp,pi B_cmp)^wedge_p[1/p].
Invert that equivalence and compose with the preceding relative comparison. Finally precompose with the canonical map from actual relative K(B_cmp,pi B_cmp). This is chi_cmp. Inverting an equivalence in the appropriate infinity category is canonical up to its contractible space of choices. Thus the character is natural and has the claimed factorization; no assertion about K_i(B_cmp,pi B_cmp) tensor Q_p has entered the argument.

6. Module compatibility.

We reconstruct this for the specific AMMN maps, rather than infer it from a bare equivalence of spectra. Set H_cmp=THH(B_cmp), a commutative algebra in cyclotomic spectra. For each auxiliary cyclotomic object T_aux used in AMMN Proposition 2.8 and Corollary 2.10, H_cmp tensor T_aux is an H_cmp-module. Every map id_{H_cmp} tensor f_aux in that construction is H_cmp-linear. Applying the lax monoidal TC functor therefore produces maps of TC(B_cmp)-modules. Circle fixed-point and Tate objects are modules over TC^-(B_cmp), hence over TC(B_cmp), and their comparison and norm maps preserve this action by (1c).

The specific steps are these. AMMN Proposition 2.8 uses Z^triv->THH(F_p) and tensors it with H_cmp. Corollary 2.10 inverts the resulting equivalence on Tate constructions; it is an equivalence of TC(B_cmp)-modules, so it can be inverted in that module category. Theorem 2.12 uses the unit map H_cmp->H_cmp tensor Z^triv and its induced rational TC equivalence. That is again a module map and its inverse is a module inverse. The subsequent map H_cmp tensor Z -> HH(B_cmp/Z) is H_cmp-linear as a circle map. It comes from the canonical commutative-algebra map to relative THH over THH(Z), hence agrees with the canonical action on algebraic HH. Projection HH(B_cmp/Z)->HH(B_cmp/W_ram) is likewise compatible with the H_cmp-action.

Definition 2.14 replaces B_cmp tensor_{sphere} F_p by B_cmp/p using their rational TC equivalence. This is induced by a map of B_cmp-algebras, and so respects TC(B_cmp). The further replacement B_cmp/p->B_cmp/pi is also a map of B_cmp-algebras. Its rational TC equivalence from (1a) is therefore an equivalence of TC(B_cmp)-modules. These are all the inversions used in the fiber-square construction.

The absolute-to-relative HC equivalence in Step 3 is induced by the circle algebra projection just described. Norm sequences give their usual negative-cyclic module actions, and this projection is linear after restricting the action along HC^-(B_cmp/Z)->HC^{-,cts}(B_cmp/W_ram).
