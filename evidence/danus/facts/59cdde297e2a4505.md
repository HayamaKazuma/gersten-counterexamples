## statement
Let A=(Z_(3)[x,y,z]/(3+xy+z^2))_(x,y,z), and let Ahat=Z_3[[x,y,z]]/(3+xy+z^2) be its maximal-ideal completion. These are ramified regular local domains of dimension three. For either R=A or R=Ahat, write {b1,b2,b3}=[b1][b2][b3] for the product of the three unit classes in actual Quillen algebraic K-theory. Then c_R=2{(1+z)/2,1+x,1+(1+x)y/4} in K_3(R) has infinite order and maps to zero in K_3(R[1/x]) integrally. Consequently K_3(R) tensor Q -> K_3(Frac(R)) tensor Q is not injective.

## proof
All K-groups without an indicated completion in this proof are actual Quillen K-groups. A relative spectrum K(D,J) means the homotopy fiber of K(D)->K(D/J), not a kernel defined only on homotopy groups.

1. External inputs and conventions.
We use the following external statements, with their precise scope.
(a) Dennis--Stein symbols in a commutative ring D are defined when 1-rs is a unit. In additive notation they satisfy <r,s>=-<s,r>, <r,s>+<r,t>=<r,s+t-rst>, and <r,st>=<rs,t>+<tr,s> whenever these symbols are defined. If r is a unit, <r,s>={r,1-rs}; if s is a unit, skew-symmetry gives <r,s>={1-rs,s}. In particular <r,1>=0. Here {b,c} is the Quillen product of the two units. Unit products are multilinear, graded commutative, and satisfy {b,1-b}=0 when both entries are units. These statements hold in actual groups and are natural under ring maps. Source: Weibel, The K-book (2013), Chapter III Definition 5.11, (D1)--(D3), and the unit-product discussion following Theorem 5.11.1. The convention 1-rs is essential.
(b) Antieau--Mathew--Morrow--Nikolaus (AMMN), On the Beilinson fiber square, arXiv:2003.12541v2, Proposition 4.10: if O is the integer ring of a complete discretely valued characteristic-zero field with perfect residue field k of characteristic p, and X is a smooth p-adic formal O-scheme, there are natural cartesian squares
K^cts(X;Q_p) -> K(X_k;Q_p)
down                   down
TC^cts(X;Q_p) -> TC(X_k;Q_p)
down                   down
HC^{-,cts}(X/O;Q_p) -> HP^cts(X/O;Q_p).
Continuous means homotopy inverse limit over the p-power reductions (Definition 4.1); ;Q_p means p-completion of the spectrum followed by inverting p. This is NOT tensoring actual homotopy groups with Q_p. Proposition 4.2, and its relative version explicitly noted before Proposition 4.10, identify the continuous Hochschild and cyclic constructions with the p-completed constructions on an algebraization of bounded p-power torsion. The proof of 4.10 identifies the horizontal fibers after projecting absolute HH to relative HH.
We also use the constructions, not an additional unasserted multiplicativity theorem, in AMMN Proposition 2.8, Corollary 2.10, Theorem 2.12, and Construction 2.14. Proposition 2.8 starts from the cyclotomic maps Z^triv -> THH(F_p) and applies them after tensoring with a bounded below cyclotomic spectrum H. Its square has TC in the top row, TC^- in the bottom row, and is cartesian; the horizontal fiber is the suspension of the circle homotopy orbits of H tensor Z_{hC_p}, completed at p. Corollary 2.10 replaces the lower-right term by the Tate construction using the equivalence induced by Z^triv -> THH(F_p), then inverts p. Theorem 2.12 specializes H=THH(D), maps THH(D) tensor Z to HH(D), and obtains the square with lower row HC^-(D)->HP(D) and upper row TC(D)->TC(D tensor_S F_p). Construction 2.14 replaces derived reduction by ordinary reduction via their rational TC equivalence. Proposition 4.10 further replaces reduction modulo p by reduction modulo a uniformizer and projects HH to HH relative to O.
(c) Blumberg--Gepner--Tabuada, Uniqueness of the multiplicative cyclotomic trace, arXiv:1103.3923, Theorems 7.3 and 7.4: the cyclotomic trace is a multiplicative natural transformation; its composite with THH is the topological Dennis trace. The projection from this trace to algebraic HH sends the class of a unit b to b^{-1} tensor b in HH_1, hence to dlog b under HKR.
(d) Nikolaus--Scholze, On topological cyclic homology, arXiv:1707.01799, Section I.3 and Corollary I.4.3: the homotopy-fixed-point and Tate constructions and their comparison have their natural lax monoidal structures; for a circle object M the norm fiber sequence is Sigma M_{hS^1}->M^{hS^1}->M^{tS^1}. Applied to modules over a circle-equivariant algebra these constructions and maps carry the induced actions from homotopy fixed points of the algebra. This last compatibility also follows directly by applying the norm construction within that module category.
Hoyois, The homotopy fixed points of the circle action on Hochschild homology, arXiv:1506.07123v2, Theorem 2.1, Lemma 2.2, Theorem 2.3: the circle-action construction on a cyclic complex is identified with its mixed complex, its cyclic/negative/periodic complexes, and the norm is the Connes B map, with sign fixed by an orientation of the circle.
(e) Bökstedt--Ottosen, A Hochschild--Kostant--Rosenberg theorem for cyclic homology, arXiv:1601.07412v2, Section 2, Definition 2.1, Remark 2.2, Proposition 2.3: for a commutative algebra over a commutative base ring, negative cyclic homology acts on cyclic homology. Its chain formula is ordinary shuffle plus the cyclic variable times cyclic shuffle. The cyclic, negative, and periodic long exact sequences are sequences of modules with the usual sign for connecting homomorphisms. These results of Section 2 do NOT require the characteristic-two hypotheses of the later sections.
(f) For a regular Noetherian scheme, Quillen K-theory is invariant under the projection from an affine-space bundle, satisfies Zariski descent, and satisfies the projective bundle formula K(P^1_k)=K(k) direct-sum K(k). Homotopy invariance and descent imply the same invariance for a Zariski locally trivial affine-line torsor. Quillen's finite-field computation gives K_(2i)(F_q)=0 and K_(2i-1)(F_q)=Z/(q^i-1) for every i>=1. Sources: Quillen, Higher algebraic K-theory I (1973), for homotopy invariance/projective bundles; Quillen, On the cohomology and K-theory of the general linear groups over a finite field (1972), for finite fields; the latter precise formula is also Weibel, The K-book, IV Corollary 1.13, following Theorem 1.12. Only K_4(F_3)=0 is needed below.

2. Ring and integral vanishing.
The ambient local ring Z_(3)[x,y,z]_(3,x,y,z) is regular of dimension four. Its element 3+xy+z^2 has nonzero image in the maximal ideal modulo its square (the image is that of 3). The quotient is regular of dimension three, with maximal ideal (x,y,z), and is a domain. Completion has the displayed presentation Ahat and is regular of dimension three for the identical parameter argument. In each, 3=-xy-z^2 is in the square of the maximal ideal. All units displayed in the statement reduce to nonzero elements of F_3.

In R set a=(1+z)/2, t=y/4, lambda=1+x, mu=1+lambda*t, and q=a(1-a). The relation of R gives q=(1-z^2)/4=1+xt. The elements a, 1-a, lambda, mu, q, and lambda*q are units. By (a),
delta=<-x,t>=<-x,t>+<-x,1>=<-x,t+1+xt>=<-x,mu>={lambda*q,mu},
because 1+x*mu=lambda*q. Graded commutativity gives 2{a,a}=0, while Steinberg gives {a,1-a}=0. Thus
2{a,q}=2{a,a}+2{a,1-a}=0.
Consequently 2[a]delta=2{a,lambda,mu}+2{a,q,mu}=c_R.
After inverting x, the first-entry formula gives delta={-x,q}; hence
c_R=2{a,-x,q}=-2{a,q,-x}=0.
This argument has taken place in integral K-groups.

3. A specified actual relative character, including its module action.
Set W=Z_3[u]/(u^2+3), with uniformizer u, and E=W[1/3]. This is an Eisenstein quadratic complete DVR with residue F_3. Let D be the 3-adic completion of a smooth W-algebra of relative dimension at most two; the cases needed are W[T] and W[X,Y,Z]/(XY+Z^2-1). Write Omega_D^j for the 3-adic completion of its relative differential j-forms, then invert 3. A restricted power series algebra W<variables> is the 3-adic completion of the polynomial algebra. u-adic and 3-adic completions agree.

Define a map chi_D from the ACTUAL spectrum K(D,uD) as follows. Map it naturally to the horizontal fiber of the top row of (b), using the compatible actual-to-continuous maps and then spectrum completion and rationalization. Map this fiber down the two squares. Identify the bottom horizontal fiber with Sigma HC^cts(D/W;Q_3) by the norm triangle of (d). This defines chi_D. Only a map out of actual relative K-theory is claimed or needed.

We verify which module action this map respects, because an arbitrary equivalence of fibers would not suffice. Fix the commutative algebra H=THH(D) in cyclotomic spectra. In the AMMN proof every object H tensor T used in 2.8 and 2.10 is an H-module by multiplication on the first factor. Every map id_H tensor f used there is H-linear. The lax monoidal TC functor gives actions of TC(H)=TC(D) on their TC images. The corresponding homotopy fixed point and Tate objects are modules over TC^-(H), hence over TC(H) via its canonical map to TC^-(H). Their norm and comparison maps preserve these actions by (d). The map of Tate objects inverted in 2.10 is one of these module maps. An equivalence of modules has an inverse in the module category; its underlying equivalence alone is not the justification. The map TC(H)->TC(H tensor Z^triv), which becomes an equivalence after rationalization in 2.12, is a module map by its construction from the unit of Z^triv. The maps H tensor Z -> HH(D) and HH(D)->HH(D/W) are maps of H-algebras after forgetting to circle objects. Derived-to-ordinary reduction D tensor_S F_3 -> D/3 and ordinary D/3 -> D/u are maps of D-algebras, so the induced equivalences on rational TC used in 2.14 and 4.10 are likewise TC(D)-linear. All these assertions survive 3-completion, rationalization, and passage to fibers. This reconstructs the bottom fiber comparison of (b) as a TC(D)-linear comparison, with the action on HC defined by restriction of the canonical map
TC(D)->TC^-(D)->HC^{-,cts}(D/W;Q_3).
The actual-to-continuous maps and the multiplicative cyclotomic trace (c) therefore make chi_D K(D)-linear, with target K(D)-action through
K(D)->HC^{-,cts}(D/W;Q_3).
The suspension sign is the ordinary module sign for a suspended fiber. No assertion of a ring structure on HC is used.

Here are the needed continuous HKR and normalization calculations explicitly. For a smooth W-algebra of relative dimension <=2 the normalized chain map from its Hochschild mixed complex is
b0 tensor ... tensor bn |-> (1/n!) b0 db1 wedge ... wedge dbn for n=0,1,2,
and zero for n>2. The factorials used are units in W. The Hochschild differential maps to zero, and the Connes operator maps to d; in degrees beyond two the latter assertion has zero target. The usual smooth HKR theorem on underlying Hochschild homology says that this map is a quasi-isomorphism, since HH_n is Omega^n and vanishes for n>2. Thus it is a quasi-isomorphism of mixed complexes to (Omega^*,0,d). This avoids an infinite rational HKR splitting.

In each total degree the cyclic, negative, or periodic total complex of this finite mixed complex has only finitely many form modules. Their reduction towers modulo 3^n are termwise surjective and the form modules are W-flat. Derived 3-completion is thus the termwise completion of the finite-in-each-degree total complexes. The relative continuity assertion in AMMN 4.2 supplies the identification with the continuous invariants; then invert 3. In particular
HC_0^cts(D/W;Q_3)=Omega_D^0,
HC_2^cts(D/W;Q_3)=(Omega_D^2/d Omega_D^1) direct-sum ker(d:Omega_D^0->Omega_D^1).
For clarity, in cyclic degree two there are exactly the terms Omega^2 in cyclic column zero and Omega^0 in cyclic column minus one; the differential on the second is d, and boundaries into the first are d Omega^1. These are homology groups in the ordinary derived category. Images are not closed before taking the quotient.

If v belongs to 1+uD, its relative unit class [v]_rel is obtained from the loop of the scalar automorphism v and its identity reduction in D/u. This construction is natural; it provides a particular element of K_1(D,uD) mapping to [v].
For D=W<T> and v=1+uT, write f=chi_D([v]_rel) in HC_0=E<T>. The composition of the norm with the projection to HH_1 sends f to df, by the mixed-complex calculation and the circle orientation in (d). The corresponding composition on the K side is the canonical Dennis trace; thus df=dlog(1+uT). Evaluation at T=0 sends the relative unit to zero, so f(0)=0. The kernel of differentiation on E<T> consists of constants: a coefficient c_n of T^n for n>=1 satisfies n*c_n=0 only if c_n=0. The convergent series log(1+uT)=sum_(n>=1) (-1)^(n+1)u^n T^n/n has this derivative and this constant term. Hence f equals that series. Substitution T=(v-1)/u, which belongs to D and defines a continuous W-algebra map from W<T>, proves chi_D([v]_rel)=log v for every such v.

For b1,b2 in D^times, K(D)-linearity and the explicit action in (e) now give
chi_D([v]_rel[b1][b2])_top = ± log(v) dlog(b1) wedge dlog(b2)
in Omega_D^2/d Omega_D^1.
To check the coefficient, use the cyclic complex with variable h of degree -2,
C^+=(E[h,h^-1]/hE[h]) tensor C_*.
The function log v is represented in column zero. A negative-cyclic representative of the unit has leading Hochschild class b^-1 tensor b, with all other terms in positive powers of h. Multiplication on column zero kills every positive-h term, including the cyclic-shuffle correction h*sh'. After multiplying once the result is still in column zero; the same observation applies to the second multiplication. The degree-two shuffle has two terms; after antisymmetrizing they both yield the same wedge, canceling the 2! in the normalized HKR map. Boundaries in the leading Hochschild representatives do not affect the resulting cyclic homology class. Thus the coefficient is one, with only the universal sign from the suspended module convention. This sign will not affect nonvanishing.

4. A global residue criterion on the formal quadric.
Take B=W<X,Y,Z>/(XY+Z^2-1). For any F in E<Z>, put omega_F=(dX/X) wedge dF initially on X invertible. It extends globally: on Z invertible, the relation XdY+YdX+2Z dZ=0 gives (dX/X) wedge dZ=-(dX wedge dY)/(2Z); the opens where X or Z is invertible cover the locus relevant to this potential pole, since X=Z=0 is impossible on the quadric.

We prove that omega_F exact in the ordinary module Omega_B^2/d Omega_B^1 implies F(1)=F(-1). Before completion, let U_+ be the complement of the line X=0,Z=-1, and U_- the complement of X=0,Z=1. Each is an affine plane:
on U_i, Z=a_i+X t_i and Y=-t_i(2a_i+X t_i), where a_+=1,a_-=-1.
Indeed U_i=D(X) union D(Z+a_i). The inverse function t_i is (Z-a_i)/X on D(X) and -Y/(Z+a_i) on D(Z+a_i); these agree. The two preimage principal opens cover the affine (X,t_i)-plane because 2a_i is a unit. This proves the isomorphism, and shows that their intersection is D(X). Completing gives two formal bidiscs with overlap W<X,X^-1,Z>.

On the i-th bidisc define q_i=-(F(Z)-F(a_i))dX/X. It is regular, because substitution Z=a_i+X t_i makes F(Z)-F(a_i) divisible by X in the restricted series ring. Its derivative is omega_F. On the overlap,
q_+-q_-=(F(1)-F(-1))dX/X.

A closed one-form A(X,t)dX+D(X,t)dt on E<X,t> has Laurent residue zero after t=c/X on E<X,X^-1>, for every c in W. To prove this write A=sum a_(m,n)X^m t^n and D=sum b_(m,n)X^m t^n with m,n>=0 and coefficients tending to zero. After substitution, the coefficient of X^-1 dX is the convergent sum
sum_(m>=0) (a_(m,m+1)-b_(m+1,m))*c^(m+1).
Closedness is partial_t A=partial_X D, so
(m+1)a_(m,m+1)=(m+1)b_(m+1,m)
for every m. Since E has characteristic zero, each difference is zero. This coefficient argument does not construct a convergent primitive by dividing an infinite series.

Now suppose omega_F=dq for a global q in Omega_B^1. The closed forms q_i-q on the bidiscs have zero residue after restriction to the common slice Z=0: on chart i this slice is t_i=-a_i/X, and -a_i is in W. Their difference is (F(1)-F(-1))dX/X and must have zero residue. Thus F(1)-F(-1)=0. This proves the criterion in the ordinary, possibly non-Hausdorff quotient.

5. Passage to B and injection for actual groups.
The W-algebra W[X,Y,Z]/(XY+Z^2-1) is smooth of relative dimension two: its partial derivatives are Y,X,2Z, whose simultaneous vanishing contradicts its equation. Substitution x=uX,y=uY,z=uZ gives a continuous homomorphism Ahat->B, since 3+u^2(XY+Z^2)=0 and every series in x,y,z converges u-adically on these images. In B continue to write
a=(1+uZ)/2, lambda=1+uX, mu=1+(1+uX)uY/4, q=a(1-a)=(1+3Z^2)/4.
All are units; a^2 reduces to 1 modulo u. Therefore the particular actual relative element
gamma=[a^2]_rel[lambda][mu] in K_3(B,uB)
maps to [a^2][lambda][mu]=2[a][lambda][mu]=c_B, the image of c_R.

Let C=B/u=F_3[X,Y,Z]/(XY+Z^2-1). To calculate its actual K_4, use a different pair of affine-plane charts, defining an affine-line torsor over P^1_F3. The first has coordinates X,t and equations Z=1+Xt, Y=-t(2+Xt). The second has coordinates Y,s and equations Z=-1-sY, X=-s(2+sY). These are the complements of the lines (X=0,Z=-1) and (Y=0,Z=1), respectively; the two omitted lines are disjoint, so the charts cover. On their intersection t is invertible and s=t^-1, while Y=-t^2 X-2t. Thus projection to t on the first chart and to s on the second glues to a map Spec C->P^1_F3 locally isomorphic to affine-line projection, with affine-linear transition of invertible slope -t^2. It is an affine-line torsor. By homotopy invariance on the two charts and their overlap and Zariski descent, the pullback on K-theory is an equivalence. The projective bundle formula and finite-field computation (f) give
K_4(C)=K_4(F_3) direct-sum K_4(F_3)=0
in actual K-theory. The long exact sequence for the actual fiber K(B,uB) now gives an injection K_3(B,uB)->K_3(B). No completed K_4 group is substituted here.

6. Character of gamma and nonzero endpoint.
Define the 3-adic logarithm of 2 by log 2=(1/2)log 4=(1/2)log(1+3). Put
L(Z)=-log 2+log(1+uZ).
The logarithm of a^2, which is in 1+uB, is 2L. The logarithm series are additive on 1+uB by the formal power-series identity and their u-adic convergence; this justifies this equality even at ramification index two. Section 3 gives the top component of chi_B(gamma) as ±tau, where
tau=2L dlog lambda wedge dlog mu.

Put t=uY/4 in this paragraph, so q=1+uX*t and mu=1+lambda*t. Differentiating lambda*q=1+uX*mu and then wedging with dlog mu proves on X invertible
dlog lambda wedge dlog mu=(dX/X) wedge dlog q-dlog q wedge dlog mu.
For completeness, the left side plus dlog q wedge dlog mu equals u dX wedge dmu/(lambda*q)=u dX wedge dt/q, which is (dX/X) wedge dq/q. These are global regular two-forms by the extension argument of Section 4.

Choose F in E<Z> with F'=6ZL/(1+3Z^2), so dF=L dlog q. Such F exists in the restricted series algebra: L and 1/(1+3Z^2) converge for |Z|<sqrt(3), and integrating coefficients divides by a positive integer, which has at most logarithmic 3-adic valuation. Hence the integrated series still has radius at least sqrt(3), in particular greater than one. It follows that
tau=2(dX/X) wedge dF-2d(F dlog mu)
as global forms. By Section 4, nonzero F(1)-F(-1) implies a nonzero top component of chi_B(gamma).

Only the odd part of L contributes to this endpoint difference. Since u^2=-3, it is
u sum_(k>=0) (-3)^k Z^(2k+1)/(2k+1).
Multiply by 6Z/(1+3Z^2), integrate termwise, and subtract the values at 1 and -1. Convergence on a disc of radius greater than one justifies all operations and regrouping by j=k+l. The result is
(F(1)-F(-1))/u = 12 sum_(j>=0) (-3)^j S_j/(2j+3),
S_j=sum_(k=0)^j 1/(2k+1).
The j=0 term is 4.

Here is a bound valid for every tail term, not only a finite calculation. Normalize v_3(3)=1. The valuation of S_j is at least -floor(log_3(2j+1)), because each denominator in its sum is at most 2j+1. Thus the valuation of the j-th summand for j>=1 is at least
A_j=1+j-floor(log_3(2j+1))-v_3(2j+3).
For j=1,2,3, substitution gives A_j=1,2,1. For j>=4, floor(log_3(2j+3))<=floor(j/2): equivalently 2j+3<3^(floor(j/2)+1).
