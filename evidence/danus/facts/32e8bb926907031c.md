## statement
Counterexample to the strict simplicial-functor assignments of Satoshi Mochizuki, Local Gersten's conjecture for regular system of parameters, arXiv:1503.07966v8 (6 August 2020), Lemma 13(III). Use the following local notation. Let B=Z_(3), g=3, t=1/4, and let C be the exact category of two-term complexes of finite free B-modules in homological degrees 1,0 with injective differential and g-annihilated cokernel, with its inherited exact sequences. Write (n,m)_B for the complex with differential diag(g id_n,id_m), where n,m are nonnegative integers. A block symbol [[a,b],[c,d]] denotes the chain map whose degree-one and degree-zero matrices are [[a,b],[gc,d]] and [[a,gb],[c,d]], respectively. Upper triangular means c=0, lower triangular means b=0. Let S_2^upper C consist of the usual exact Ar[2] diagrams with zero diagonal whose every structure map is upper triangular; Ar[2] consists of the pairs (i,j), 0<=i<=j<=2, ordered componentwise. Let i_lower S_2^upper C have these objects and natural isomorphisms with lower triangular components. Let Bcal be the exact category of two-term complexes of finite B-modules of projective dimension at most one. Define assignments mu'_1 and mu'_2 by (n,m)_B -> [B --g--> B]^n and (n,m)_B -> [B --1--> B]^n, respectively, and by sending the block symbol [[a,b],[c,d]] to a in both degrees. There exist objects X_1,X_t of S_2^upper C and a morphism F:X_1->X_t in i_lower S_2^upper C such that each mu'_i sends both objects to valid S_2 Bcal diagrams and every component of F to an isomorphism, but the componentwise image of F is not a natural transformation, for each i=1,2. Consequently these assignments do not induce the degree-two functors, and hence not the simplicial functors, claimed in Lemma 13(III). This statement does not assert that Theorem 5 of that paper is false.

## proof
# Audit of the strict simplicial assignment in arXiv:1503.07966v8

Scope: Satoshi Mochizuki, *Local Gersten's conjecture for regular system of parameters*, arXiv:1503.07966v8, dated 6 August 2020, Conventions 11, Definition 12, and Lemma 13(III). This draft is a candidate proof pending the Danus verifier. Its conclusion concerns the displayed strict functor assignment, not the truth of Theorem 5.

The exact PDF was downloaded to `downloads/mochizuki_1503.07966v8.pdf`, extracted to the corresponding `.txt`, and read, including the proof of Lemma 13. Two Matlas queries returned unrelated results; the source was verified directly at https://arxiv.org/html/1503.07966v8 .

## Definitions and precise claim

Set B=Z_(3), g=3, and t=1/4. The exact category C in the paper has two-term complexes of finite free B-modules in homological degrees 1 and 0, with injective differential and cokernel annihilated by g. Its exact sequences are the inherited exact sequences of complexes. The standard object (n,m)_B has differential diag(g id_n,id_m). In Conventions 11, a block symbol phi=[[a,b],[c,d]] denotes the chain map with degree-one matrix [[a,b],[g c,d]] and degree-zero matrix [[a,g b],[c,d]]. Upper triangular means c=0; lower triangular means b=0. Empty rectangular blocks are zero maps.

Let Ar[2] be the poset of pairs (i,j) with 0<=i<=j<=2, ordered componentwise. An S_2 object is an Ar[2] diagram with zero diagonal and exact sequences X(i,j)->X(i,k)->X(j,k). Definition 12 requires every diagram arrow to be upper triangular. The category i_lower S_2^upper C has these objects and natural isomorphisms whose components are lower triangular.

The two assignments preceding Definition 12 send (n,m)_B to Typ(g)^n and Typ(1)^n, respectively, and send phi to the chain map a in both degrees. Lemma 13(III) asserts that applying these assignments to diagrams and their component maps gives simplicial functors into i S_* Bcal, where Bcal is the paper's category of two-term complexes in modules of projective dimension at most one. The claim to prove here is that BOTH assignments fail to send an actual morphism of i_lower S_2^upper C to a morphism of i S_2 Bcal.

## The complete S_2 objects

Put G=[B --g--> B]=(1,0)_B, I=[B --1--> B]=(0,1)_B, M=G direct-sum I=(1,1)_B, and 0=(0,0)_B. These lie in C: their differentials are injective, and their cokernels are respectively B/gB, zero, B/gB, and zero. For b in {1,t}, set

    (alpha_b)_1=(b,1)^T,     (alpha_b)_0=(g b,1)^T,
    (beta_b)_1=(1,-b),       (beta_b)_0=(1,-g b).

The equations diag(g,1)(alpha_b)_1=(alpha_b)_0 and g(beta_b)_1=(beta_b)_0 diag(g,1) prove that these are chain maps I->M and M->G. In both degrees take s:G->M to be (1,0)^T and r:M->I to be (0,1). They are chain maps. In both degrees,

    beta_b alpha_b=0, beta_b s=id_G, r alpha_b=id_I,
    r s=0, alpha_b r+s beta_b=id_M.

Indeed the last identity adds [[0,b],[0,1]] to [[1,-b],[0,0]] in degree one and replaces b by g b in degree zero. Thus 0->I->M->G->0 is split exact as a sequence of complexes, so it is an admissible exact sequence in C.

Define X_b(0,1)=I, X_b(0,2)=M, X_b(1,2)=G, and X_b(i,i)=0 for i=0,1,2. The arrow (0,1)->(0,2) is alpha_b and (0,2)->(1,2) is beta_b. The arrow (0,1)->(1,2) is zero, consistent both with beta_b alpha_b=0 and with factoring through (1,1). Every arrow with a zero source or target is the unique zero map. Each endomorphism is the identity of its vertex. This exhausts the arrows: among distinct nonzero vertices there are exactly the three comparable pairs I->M, M->G, and I->G. All other distinct comparable pairs involve a zero vertex. It is a functor because the sole composition through three nonzero vertices is beta_b alpha_b=0; any competing factorization is through a zero vertex. The exactness requirements with three distinct indices give the split sequence just proved; repeated indices give 0->Y->Y or Y->Y->0 with identity as the nonzero arrow, for Y in {0,I,M,G}. Thus X_b belongs to S_2 C.

The block symbol for alpha_b has upper-right block b and lower-right block 1, with its two left blocks having zero columns; hence its lower-left block is zero. The block symbol for beta_b has upper-left block 1 and upper-right block -b, with its two bottom blocks having zero rows; hence its lower-left block is zero. The I->G zero arrow has zero lower-left block, as do all arrows involving the zero object. The identity of each vertex has zero off-diagonal blocks. This checks EVERY structure arrow, including the quotient beta_b and all zero arrows: X_b belongs to S_2^upper C.

## A lower triangular natural isomorphism

Define F:X_1->X_t with components ell=4 id_I at (0,1), v at (0,2), q=t id_G at (1,2), and the unique identity of zero at each diagonal vertex, where

    v_1=[[1,0],[3,1]],     v_0=[[1,0],[1,1]].

The chain-map identity is diag(3,1)v_1=v_0 diag(3,1)=[[3,0],[3,1]]. Both v matrices have inverses obtained by negating their lower-left entries. Both 4 and t are units of B. Thus every component is a chain isomorphism.

Naturality for alpha follows from

    v_1(1,1)^T=(1,4)^T=(t,1)^T 4,
    v_0(3,1)^T=(3,4)^T=(3t,1)^T 4.

Naturality for beta follows from

    (1,-t)v_1=(t,-t)=t(1,-1),
    (1,-3t)v_0=(t,-3t)=t(1,-3).

Naturality for every remaining arrow is the equality of zero maps or the identity naturality equation, by the exhaustive classification of arrows above. Therefore F is a natural isomorphism of the complete Ar[2] diagrams.

In Conventions 11 the block symbol of v is [[1,0],[1,1]], hence lower triangular; its inverse has symbol [[1,0],[-1,1]], also lower triangular. On I, ell has empty upper blocks, hence zero upper-right block; on G, q has empty right blocks, hence zero upper-right block. Their inverses have the same empty-block property. The components at zero also have zero upper-right blocks. Thus F is a morphism of i_lower S_2^upper C (and its inverse is one as well).

## Failure of naturality after applying either assignment

Fix i in {1,2} and write H=G for i=1, H=I for i=2. On vertices, mu'_i(I)=0 and mu'_i(M)=mu'_i(G)=H. It sends alpha_b to 0->H and beta_b to id_H, since the relevant upper-left block of beta_b is 1. Each X_b therefore maps to the valid split S_2 object 0->0->H --id--> H->0.

On the proposed morphism's components it sends ell to id_0, v to id_H, and q to t id_H. The right-hand naturality square would demand

    mu'_i(beta_t) mu'_i(v) = mu'_i(q) mu'_i(beta_1),

whose left side is id_H and whose right side is t id_H. These are unequal chain maps: in degree zero they send 1 in B to 1 and 1/4, and 1-1/4=3/4 is nonzero in B. Thus the componentwise image is not a natural transformation, even though its individual components are isomorphisms and its source and target diagrams are valid S_2 objects. Neither assignment defines the asserted degree-two functor, hence neither defines the asserted simplicial functor.

The proof of Lemma 13(III) checks composition within the upper triangular class and within the lower triangular class. The naturality square here mixes the two classes. The missing mixed compatibility is exactly what fails. No assertion about the truth or falsity of Theorem 5 follows from this calculation alone, and no Gersten counterexample or prior project fact is used.

## intuition
Upper-left block projection respects compositions within either triangular class, but an S_2 morphism also requires naturality across squares with upper triangular horizontal maps and lower triangular vertical maps. The proposed exact diagrams give a mixed square where the discarded g times off-diagonal product is nonzero.
