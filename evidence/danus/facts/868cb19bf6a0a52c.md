## statement
Scalar Frobenius classes survive generic Cohen completion. Let p be a prime, k an algebraic closure of F_p, W=W(k) the Witt ring, K=W[1/p], and D a smooth affine W-algebra of relative dimension one whose special fiber D_0=D/pD is connected and nonempty. Put U=Spec(D_0), S={s in D: s mod p is nonzero}, Dhat=lim_n D/p^nD, C=lim_n S^(-1)D/p^nD, and F=C[1/p]. Write Ddagger for the weak p-adic completion of D and use continuous differential forms on the completed rings. Let d>=1, q=p^d, and let phi:Ddagger→Ddagger be an integral Frobenius lift restricting to the d-th Witt Frobenius on W and reducing to the q-th power map on D_0. If omega in Omega^1_(Ddagger/W)[1/p] has nonzero class in H^1_rig(U/K) and phi^*omega-a omega=dh for some a in W and h in Ddagger[1/p], then the image of omega in Omega^1_(C/W)[1/p]/dF is nonzero. The quotient is by the ordinary image dF, without topological closure.

## proof
We give all generic-completion steps explicitly. A smooth connected curve has no intersecting irreducible components, because its local rings are domains. Thus D_0 is an integral normal domain. Set L=Frac(D_0).

1. The integral intersection. The localization S^(-1)D at pD is a regular local ring of dimension one, with maximal ideal generated by p and residue field L. Hence C is a complete discrete valuation ring with uniformizer p, residue field L, and no p-torsion. The p-torsion-free ring Dhat embeds in C. Indeed, for every n>=1 the map D/p^nD→S^(-1)D/p^nD is injective: if sa belongs to p^nD with s in S, reduction modulo p gives a in pD since D_0 is a domain. Write a=pa_1, cancel p by W-flatness of D, and repeat n times. Taking limits gives the embedding and the integral intersection Dhat∩p^nC=p^nDhat. Continuous forms on C are the p-adic completion of the forms on S^(-1)D.

2. The continuous constant field. We prove ker(d:F→Omega^1_(C/W)[1/p])=K. Smoothness provides, on a neighborhood of the generic special point obtained by inverting an element of S, an etale coordinate t and a ring D' etale over W[t]. The image of t in L is a separating parameter. Completing the localization of D' at its generic special point again gives C.

The map W[t]→C[[z]] with t↦t+z extends, by formal etaleness and successively modulo powers of z, to a Taylor homomorphism D'→C[[z]] reducing at z=0 to the given D'→C. The Taylor series of every further denominator has a unit constant coefficient in C, so the map extends over the localization S^(-1)D' and then over C by p-adic completeness, coefficient by coefficient. Denote its coefficients by H_j:C→C. They are integral W-linear p-adically continuous Hasse operators. Uniqueness of the etale lift shows that successive translations by z and w equal translation by z+w, so j!H_j=partial^j, where partial=H_1 and partial(t)=1. The continuous cotangent module of C over W is free on dt.

If f in C has df=0, then partial(f)=0 and j!H_j(f)=0 for every j>0. Since C is torsion-free over Z, H_j(f)=0. On reduction modulo p all positive Hasse operators annihilate fbar in L. For each r>=1 the elements 1,t,...,t^(p^r-1) form a basis of L over L^(p^r): since L/k(t) is finite separable, the degree formula and Frobenius give [L:L^p]=p, and t is not a p-th power because dt is nonzero. Iteration gives [L:L^(p^r)]=p^r. The purely inseparable minimal polynomial of t over L^(p^r) has degree p^r, since any smaller degree would make t a p-th power in L. Write fbar=sum_(i=0)^(p^r-1)b_i^(p^r)t^i. The Taylor series of each b_i^(p^r) has no terms in degrees 1,...,p^r-1. Comparing successively the coefficients of z^(p^r-1), z^(p^r-2), down to z shows b_i=0 for i>0. Thus fbar lies in L^(p^r) for every r.

The intersection of these power subfields is k. To see this, the valuation of an element in that intersection at every point of the smooth proper model of L is divisible by every p^r, hence is zero. Such an element is a global regular function on a proper connected curve over k, therefore is in k. Conversely k is perfect. Choose a lift w_0 in W of fbar. Then (f-w_0)/p is again integral and has differential zero. Repeating produces w_i in W and an expansion f=sum_(i>=0)p^i w_i. This series converges in W as well as in C, so f belongs to W. Scaling by a power of p proves the assertion for F.

3. Scalar pole removal. The integral Frobenius phi fixes p and extends to Dhat by continuity. Its reduction maps nonzero residues to their q-th powers, so it also extends to C, by inverting S and then completing.

We claim that f in C and phi(f)-af=r in Dhat imply f in Dhat. Reduction gives fbar^q-abar fbar=rbar in D_0. A pole of fbar at a closed point of U of order m>0 would contribute pole order qm to the first term and at most m to the second. Since q>1 these cannot cancel. Thus fbar has no poles on U. Normality of the affine curve implies fbar belongs to D_0. Choose g_0 in D lifting fbar, set f_1=(f-g_0)/p, and set r_1=(r-phi(g_0)+ag_0)/p. The numerator of r_1 lies in Dhat∩pC=pDhat, so r_1 lies in Dhat, and phi(f_1)-af_1=r_1.

Induction gives g_i in D, f_i in C, r_i in Dhat, and
 f=sum_(i=0)^(n-1)p^i g_i+p^n f_n.
The partial sums converge in Dhat and p^n f_n tends to zero in C. The resulting element of Dhat maps to f. This proves the claim without any assumption that phi is W-linear.

4. Nonvanishing. Suppose omega=df for f in F. Then d(phi(f)-af-h)=0, so step 2 gives phi(f)-af=h+c for c in K. Multiply all of f,h,c,omega by one sufficiently large power of p to make f integral in C, h integral in Dhat, and c integral in W. The equation remains valid because phi(p)=p. Step 3 gives f in Dhat. Undoing the scaling, omega is exact in the rational completed de Rham complex of Dhat.

The precise external theorem used to exclude this is Daniel Caro and Marco D'Addezio, Injectivity failure in crystalline comparisons, arXiv:2511.11444, Theorem 3.2.1: for every smooth k-scheme X and every overconvergent F-isocrystal Mdagger, the comparison H^1_rig(X,Mdagger)→H^1_conv(X,M) is injective, with M the underlying convergent F-isocrystal. Apply it to X=U and the constant rank-one Frobenius coefficient. The field k is perfect, U is smooth, and that coefficient is overconvergent. For this smooth affine lift, rigid cohomology is computed by the rational weakly completed de Rham complex and convergent cohomology by the rational completed de Rham complex; the comparison is induced by Ddagger→Dhat. The latter description is the formal smooth de Rham/crystalline comparison, recorded also in Theorem 2.1.2 of the same paper, with its convergent convention. In degree one both descriptions use the ordinary image of the differential. Thus the primitive constructed above contradicts the assumed nonzero rigid class and injectivity.

Only the original finite-type affine curve U is used in the published injectivity theorem. No general generic-point full-faithfulness or commutation of cohomology with an infinite localization is assumed.

## intuition
Frobenius magnifies every pole, forcing a putative generic-completed primitive to lie on the original completed affine curve, where degree-one comparison is injective.
