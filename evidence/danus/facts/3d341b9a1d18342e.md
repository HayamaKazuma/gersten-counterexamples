## statement
The ring A=(Z_(5)[T,x,y]/(Phi_5(1+y)+x^4+Txy^3))_(5,x,y), where Phi_5(z)=z^4+z^3+z^2+z+1, is a regular local ring of dimension two with residue field F_5(T). There is an actual infinite-order class c∈K_3(A) whose image in K_3(Frac A) is zero. Here Z_(5) is the localization of the integers at the prime (5), T,x,y are indeterminates, Frac denotes the fraction field, and K_3 denotes Quillen algebraic K-theory.

## proof
This proof uses the verified geometric persistence fact 9a4571503438e4d4 as its sole mathematical predecessor. We first state the precise external K-theory and regulator inputs and then apply them to the rings in the statement.

External inputs. (a) For a regular noetherian ring R, the map from K-theory of finitely generated projective modules to G-theory of finitely generated modules is an equivalence. A finite ring map R→D gives a pushforward G(D)→G(R) by restriction of scalars. If R→R' is flat, tensor product is exact, and its natural isomorphism with restriction of scalars gives the flat-base-change identity for this pushforward and D'=D⊗_R R'. For an element alpha∈K_n(R') and the closed immersion j:Spec D'→Spec R', the projection formula is j_*(j^*alpha) = alpha·[D'] in G_n(R'). These statements are Quillen's resolution and functoriality theorems, and the tensor identity (M⊗_(D') (D'⊗_(R') P))≅M⊗_(R')P; see Weibel, The K-book (2013), V.3.3, V.3.3.2, V.3.7.2 and V.3.12. All uses below involve finite closed immersions between regular affine rings, and flat tensor maps, so no properness or Tor-independence condition is missing. Restriction to an open disjoint from a closed immersion annihilates its G-theory pushforward, since the corresponding finitely generated module localizes to zero.

(b) K_n commutes with filtered colimits of rings. In particular, if H is a multiplicative subset of a ring R, K_n(H^(-1)R)=colim_(h∈H) K_n(R[1/h]). This is Weibel IV.6.4: one uses the exact categories of finite idempotent matrices for projective modules, which commute with filtered colimits, as do Quillen's Q construction and its homotopy groups. Thus an equality between classes from a fixed finite stage in a filtered localization holds after some finite further localization.

(c) For a number field L with ring of integers O_L and r_2 complex-conjugate pairs of embeddings, Borel's theorem gives dim_R(K_3(O_L)⊗R)=r_2. The analytic Beilinson regulator on this group is injective after tensoring with R, and it is obtained at every embedding tau:L→C as a value in C/R(2), where R(p)=(2πi)^p R. Here K_3 corresponds to weight p=2. Its values at conjugate embeddings are complex conjugates. Sources: Borel, Stable real cohomology of arithmetic groups (1974), Proposition 12.2, for the rank; Bunke–Tamme, Multiplicative differential algebraic K-theory and applications, arXiv:1311.1421v3, Section 6, particularly equation (6.4) and its proof, for injectivity and comparison with Borel's regulator. In that source the comparison is up to a nonzero factor 2, which has no effect on nonvanishing. The local definitions in Section 6 identify the target at a point as C/(2πi)^p R, with representatives i^(p+1)dt in its interval model and with conjugate-embedding invariance. In weight two there are only complex-place coordinates; their representatives are purely imaginary. These are statements for spectra of number rings, and below they are first applied exactly to such a ring.

(d) For every regular separated scheme X of finite type over Z, Bunke–Tamme's Theorem 2.31 gives a natural regulator of ring spectra whose homotopy maps are
 K_n(X)→ product_(p≥0) H_D,an^(2p−n)(X(C),R(p)),
with the arithmetic complex-conjugation invariance. Projection to a single complex component is allowed after defining this map. The source agrees with Quillen K-theory by their Remark 2.27. Remark 2.6 and Definition 2.7 identify the target with the shifted cone of
 (2πi)^p A_R^•(U) ⊕ F^p A_C^•(U) → A_C^•(U), (a,b)↦a−b,
for a smooth complex manifold U arising from X. Here A_C denotes smooth complex differential forms, A_R real forms, and F^p consists of forms with at least p holomorphic differentials. No logarithmic condition at infinity is imposed. For multiplication one uses forms omega on [0,1]×U with omega|_0 real-twisted and omega|_1 in F^p; their product is the wedge product. Proposition 2.8 maps this interval model to the cone by (omega|_0,omega|_1,−integral_[0,1] omega), and Corollary 2.14 establishes this multiplicative structure. Their Definitions 2.16–2.20 show that the weight-one class of a line bundle with a unitary Chern connection is represented in the interval model by the constant form 2πi e, where e is a real closed two-form representing the topological first Chern class. Indeed its curvature is of type (1,1), its connection is already unitary, and the degree-two part of their unnormalized Chern character Tr exp(−curvature) is 2πi e. These observations also verify the specific normalization of the product used below.

1. The source ring and supported class. Define P(y)=Phi_5(1+y)=5+10y+10y^2+5y^3+y^4 and F=P(y)+x^4+Txy^3. Let R_0=Z_(5)[T,x,y]/(F), m=(5,x,y), and S=R_0\m, so A=S^(-1)R_0. The ambient regular local ring Z_(5)[T,x,y]_(5,x,y) has dimension three and residue field F_5(T). The class of F in its cotangent space is 5, which is nonzero. Hence A is regular local of dimension two with the stated residue field. Explicitly,
 5(1+2y+2y^2+y^3)=−(x^4+y^4+Txy^3)
shows its maximal ideal is (x,y), since the parenthesis is a unit. It is a domain; alternatively this and its fraction field Q(x,y) follow from predecessor 9a4571503438e4d4, which proves the defining equation primitive and irreducible as a polynomial in T.

Fix a primitive fifth root rho, and define E=Q(rho), W=Z_(5)[rho], u=rho−1. Predecessor 9a4571503438e4d4 proves W is a DVR with uniformizer u and fraction field E, and proves the flat splitting construction below. The quotient A/x is the DVR W[T]_(u), under y↦u. This follows by first taking R_0/(x)=W[T] and then localizing at the image (u) of m. Denote Spec(A/x) by D, and its closed immersion into Spec A by i.

Let O_E be the ring of integers of E. Since W is integrally closed with fraction field E, O_E⊂W: every element integral over Z is integral over W and hence belongs to W. The four embeddings of E are tau sigma_g, g∈G={1,2,3,4}=(Z/5Z)^×, where tau is any fixed embedding and sigma_g(rho)=rho^g. E has no real embeddings and two complex pairs. By input (c), there exists gamma_0∈K_3(O_E) whose regulator is nonzero: otherwise the injective regulator from the two-dimensional real vector space K_3(O_E)⊗R would be zero. Define
 beta_0=gamma_0−sigma_(−1)(gamma_0)∈K_3(O_E),
and let beta be its image in K_3(W). At every embedding the point-regulator value of sigma_(−1)(gamma_0) is the complex conjugate of that of gamma_0. Since R(2)=R as a real subspace of C, the unique imaginary representative changes sign under conjugation. Thus the regulator of beta_0 is twice that of gamma_0 and is nonzero. The same holds after restriction to E. We have sigma_(−1)beta=−beta exactly as an equality of actual integral K_3 classes.

Pull beta to K_3(D), and set c=i_*(beta|_D)∈K_3(A), using input (a). Both A and D are regular noetherian. The restriction of c to A[1/x] is zero because the defining finite-module pushforward is supported on x=0. The map to Frac A factors through A[1/x]; therefore c has zero generic image. It remains to show that no nonzero integer kills c.

2. A finite-stage detector. Set B=A⊗_(Z_(5))W and mathcal T=Spec B[1/u,1/y]. The map A→B[1/u,1/y] is flat. Define
 Qcal=Spec E[T,x,y,y^(-1)]/(F)
     ≅Spec E[x,v,y,y^(-1)]/(xv+P(y)),
where v=x^3+Ty^3 and T=(v−x^3)/y^3. For each g∈G put a_g=rho^g−1, D_g=V(x,y−a_g) in Qcal, J_g=O_Qcal(−D_g), and eta_g=1−[J_g]∈K_0(Qcal). Since D_g is an effective Cartier divisor, the two-term resolution 0→J_g→O_Qcal→O_(D_g)→0 identifies eta_g with [O_(D_g)].

By predecessor 9a4571503438e4d4, Qcal is smooth, mathcal T=Spec S^(-1)O(Qcal), and for each s∈S the smooth complex surface U_s=Qcal_s(C) satisfies: the four real classes
 e_g=c_1^top(O(D_g))∈H^2(Qcal(C),R)
have exactly the diagonal relation, and H^2(Qcal(C),R)→H^2(U_s,R) is injective. Define the actual class
 kappa=sum_(g∈G) (sigma_g beta|_E)·eta_g ∈K_3(Qcal),
using the pullback of K_3(E) along the structure morphism.

We now prove that kappa|_(Qcal_s) has a nonzero regulator for every s∈S. This regulator is defined at a finite arithmetic stage, not by assuming that Qcal itself is of finite type over Z. Namely choose an integer N divisible by 5 and all denominators of s. Over O_E[1/N] the four roots a_g and their pairwise differences are units, because Norm_(E/Q)(rho^g−1)=5, as follows by multiplying its four conjugates and using Phi_5(1)=5; differences have the form rho^h(rho^(g−h)−1), so their norms are also 5. The affine scheme
 X_N=Spec O_E[1/N][x,v,y,y^(-1)]/(xv+P(y))
is smooth over O_E[1/N]. If x or v is nonzero in a residue field the corresponding partial derivative is nonzero; if both vanish, the four distinct roots of P ensure P'(y) nonzero. Hence X_N and its principal open (X_N)_s are regular separated schemes of finite type over Z. The root divisors extend as Cartier divisors there: near y=a_g the product of all other factors y−a_h is a unit, and xv=−P(y) expresses y−a_g as x times a regular function, so their ideal is generated by x. The coefficient classes sigma_g beta_0 also extend there, so kappa does too. Apply input (d) at this stage and project to the component selected by tau. On increasing N the construction is compatible by naturality; the chosen complex component is always U_s. Input (b), applied to the filtered union of O_E[1/N], therefore defines a regulator on the whole group K_3(Qcal_s), so nonzero regulator truly detects a class of that group.

For each g let r_g be the unique purely imaginary representative in C of the weight-two point regulator of sigma_g(beta|_E) at tau. All the embeddings of E occur among tau sigma_g. The nonzero regulator of beta_0 therefore implies some r_g≠0. Since G is abelian and sigma_(−1)beta=−beta, we have r_(−g)=−r_g. Thus the vector (r_g) is nonzero and not diagonal.

For every complex surface U, F^3 A_C^•(U)=0 literally, because there are only two holomorphic coordinate differentials. The cone description in (d) and its long exact sequence give
 H_D,an^3(U,R(3))=H^2(U,C)/H^2(U,R(3)).
Indeed the possible next term is the kernel of H^3(U,R(3))→H^3(U,C), which is zero by extension of scalars from real to complex cohomology. This argument applies to every U_s without properness assumptions.

A point class represented by r_g∈C/R(2) has interval representative −r_g dt. Choose a Hermitian metric on O(D_g) and its Chern connection; write omega_g for a real closed two-form representing e_g. Its weight-one regulator is represented by the interval-constant form 2πi omega_g. In weight one, eta_g=1−[O(−D_g)] has the same class as O(D_g), since first Chern class is additive. Wedge multiplying gives −2πi r_g dt∧omega_g. Under the map −integral_[0,1] in input (d) its cone component is 2πi r_g omega_g. Consequently the weight-three regulator of kappa restricted to U_s is
 [2πi sum_g r_g e_g|_(U_s)] ∈ H^2(U_s,C)/H^2(U_s,R(3)).
There are no other weight contributions to this expression: the regulator of K_3 of a zero-dimensional complex scheme is zero in every weight other than two, since its point Deligne cohomology is nonzero only in degrees zero and one and 2p−3=1 forces p=2.

Write r_g=i t_g with t_g∈R. The vector (t_g) is not diagonal, so the relation computation in 9a4571503438e4d4 gives sum_g t_g e_g≠0. Its image remains nonzero on U_s by the same fact's restriction injectivity. Therefore
 2πi sum_g r_g e_g|_(U_s)=−2π sum_g t_g e_g|_(U_s)
is a nonzero real cohomology class. In contrast H^2(U_s,R(3)) is the purely imaginary real subspace iH^2(U_s,R) of H^2(U_s,C). The intersection of the real and purely imaginary subspaces is zero. The displayed Deligne regulator is thus nonzero. Since its target is a real vector space, the class kappa|_(Qcal_s) has infinite order.

3. Identification with the supported class and continuity. After the flat map A→B[1/u,1/y], the divisor x=0 splits as the disjoint union of the four closed subschemes D_g∩mathcal T, because P(y)=product_g(y−a_g) has pairwise distinct roots in E. On the g-th component the original coefficient map W→A/x→O(D_g∩mathcal T) sends rho=1+y to rho^g. It is therefore the composite of sigma_g:W→W with W⊂E and the structure map from E. Flat base change in input (a) identifies the pullback of c with the sum of the pushforwards of these coefficient classes. On each component sigma_g(beta|_E) is the restriction of a class from K_3(mathcal T). The projection formula therefore gives
 c|_(mathcal T)=sum_g (sigma_g beta|_E)[O_(D_g∩mathcal T)]
              =kappa|_(mathcal T).
Here mathcal T is regular, being a localization of the smooth E-surface Qcal, so G=K is legitimate at both ends. No identification of G-theory with K-theory on the singular ring B is made.

By input (b),
 K_3(mathcal T)=colim_(s∈S) K_3(Qcal_s).
If a nonzero integer n killed c in K_3(A), then it would kill kappa in this colimit. The equality n kappa=0 would hold at some finite stage Qcal_s. This contradicts the nonzero real-vector-space regulator of kappa at every such stage. Therefore c is an actual infinite-order element of K_3(A), and its generic image is zero as already proved.

## intuition
A coefficient K_3 class from Q(zeta_5), pushed from x=0, is supported and hence generically zero. After a flat splitting extension, it becomes a sum of number-field coefficients times four divisor classes. Their regulator is nonzero on every allowed finite localization by the verified exceptional-curve Picard and Betti persistence theorem.
