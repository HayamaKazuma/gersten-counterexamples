## statement
Relative cyclic detection for an essentially smooth completed surface. Let k be an algebraic closure of F_5, let W_0=W(k), and let R be the ring of integers in a finite totally ramified extension of W_0[1/5], with uniformizer pi and fraction field E. Let B_0 be a localization of a smooth R-algebra with relative cotangent module finite projective of constant rank two, and put B=lim_n B_0/5^nB_0. Suppose B is nonzero. For a ring T and ideal I, let K(T,I) be the homotopy fiber of K(T)→K(T/I), and write mathcalK(T,I)=K(T,I)^wedge_5[1/5] for spectral completion followed by inversion of 5. There is a natural equivalence mathcalK(B,pi B)≃Sigma HC^cts(B/R;Q_5), compatible with the K(B)-module action through the negative cyclic Chern character. The degree-two cyclic group has a canonical top-form summand Omega^2_(B/R)[1/5]/d Omega^1_(B/R)[1/5], with continuous relative forms and ordinary differential image. If beta is an actual element of K_3(R,pi R), its relative point coordinate b lies in E under mathcalK(R,pi R)≃Sigma HC^cts(R/R;Q_5). For every invertible B-module J, the top-form component of beta_B·(1-[J]) is -b c_1^dR(J). Thus nonvanishing of that form, or of a finite sum of such forms, proves nonvanishing of the corresponding actual relative K-class after rationalized spectral 5-completion. This does not assert any injection of actual K-theory into its completion.

## proof
We specify both the extension of the fiber theorem to this ring and the product calculation.

1. External statements used. Antieau–Mathew–Morrow–Nikolaus, On the Beilinson fiber square, arXiv:2003.12541, Theorem A: for every commutative ring T henselian along (5), the natural square with top row K(T;Q_5)→K(T/5;Q_5) and bottom row HC^-(T;Q_5)→HP(T;Q_5) is homotopy cartesian; consequently K(T,(5);Q_5)≃Sigma HC(T;Q_5). Their coefficient Q_5 means completion of spectra at 5 and then inversion of 5, rather than tensoring the actual groups with Q_5. Proposition 4.2 says that for the completion of a quasi-compact quasi-separated scheme with bounded 5-power torsion, HH, THH, HC^-, HP, and TC agree after 5-completion with the inverse limits of their values on the infinitesimal thickenings. The text immediately before Proposition 4.10 explicitly extends this statement to relative Hochschild and cyclic theories. Corollary 3.8 says that for a ring in which 5 is nilpotent and a nilpotent ideal, the relative K- and TC-spectra are quasi-isogenous to zero; in particular their rationalized 5-completions vanish. We will not apply Proposition 4.10 by claiming that B is topologically finitely presented over R. Instead we repeat its relative-Hochschild argument with uniform finite-range bounds.

2. Absolute to relative Hochschild homology. Both B_0 and B are 5-torsion-free. The latter follows from flatness of B_0 over the discrete valuation ring R and its adic completion. They therefore satisfy the bounded-torsion condition of Proposition 4.2, so they compute the same completed Hochschild and cyclic objects; the continuous relative forms are the adic completions of Omega^j_(B_0/R). Completion is exact on their finite projective B_0-modules, and Omega^j vanishes for j>2.

We first show the base cotangent complex has bounded torsion after completion. For every n, W_0/5^n is the filtered union of finite etale Witt extensions of Z/5^n. The relative cotangent complex of that union over Z/5^n is zero. By flat base change this implies L_(W_0/Z)^wedge_5=0. Write R=W_0[pi] using an Eisenstein polynomial f. The complete cotangent complex L_(R/Z)^wedge_5 is represented in cohomological degrees -1,0 by [R --f'(pi)→ R]. As the characteristic-zero extension is separable, f'(pi) is nonzero. Choose an integer a>=0 with 5^a in f'(pi)R. Multiplication by 5^a on this two-term complex is null-homotopic.

Completed cotangent transitivity for B_0 gives a triangle M→Lhat→P, with
 M=[B --f'(pi)→B], Lhat=L_(B_0/Z)^wedge_5, P=Omega^1_(B_0/R)^wedge_5.
Here P is finite projective and in degree zero, whereas M is in degrees -1,0. Hence the connecting morphism P→M[1] vanishes and the triangle splits as one of B-module complexes. The kernel terms in the i-th derived exterior power of the projection Lhat→P are sums of
 (L Lambda^r M) tensor Lambda^(i-r)P, 1<=r<=i.
They are killed as objects by 5^(ar): the r-th derived exterior power sends a null-homotopy of 5^a id_M to a null-homotopy of 5^(ar) on that exterior power.

For completeness the Hochschild–Kostant–Rosenberg filtration on derived HH of a commutative ring over its base has graded pieces (L Lambda^i L)[i], and its tails are increasingly connective. The corresponding filtration here compares absolute HH(B_0/Z) and relative HH(B_0/R). In any fixed homotopy degree range there are only finitely many graded pieces relevant to this comparison. This remains so after derived 5-completion: completion preserves connectivity, since for a c-connective spectrum the only possible extra bottom homotopy group is the inverse-limit-one of pi_c/5^n, and that quotient tower is surjective. The finite collection of exterior-power bounds, combined along the finite sequence of extensions, therefore bounds the 5-power torsion of the comparison cone in any fixed range. It follows that
 HH(B_0;Z_5)[1/5]→HH(B_0/R;Z_5)[1/5]
is an equivalence of spectra with circle action.

Ordinary cyclic homology is the circle homotopy-orbit construction on HH. For connective spectra, its simplicial bar construction has a skeletal filtration whose omitted part is arbitrarily connective. Each finite stage involves finite colimits and tensor products with finite cell spaces, which commute with spectral completion. The same finite-range argument therefore proves an equivalence on rationalized completed ordinary cyclic homology. Equivalently, by the circle norm fiber sequence, the square from absolute to relative HC^-→HP is cartesian. No claim is needed that its separate HC^- and HP vertical maps are equivalences, since those constructions involve infinite limits.

3. The fiber equivalence. The complete ring B is henselian along (5), so Theorem A applies to the ordinary ring B. The previous step replaces the horizontal fiber of the absolute cyclic row by the relative cyclic fiber over R. Moreover pi is topologically nilpotent, and B/5→B/pi has nilpotent kernel, because 5 is a unit times a power of pi. Corollary 3.8 makes the induced map of rationalized completed K-spectra an equivalence. Exactness of completion and inversion of 5 therefore identifies mathcalK(B,pi B) with mathcalK(B,5B). We obtain the stated equivalence with Sigma HC^cts(B/R;Q_5). Precomposition with the canonical map from actual relative K-theory gives the actual character.

4. Module compatibility. We record why the necessary module structure is present, instead of deriving it merely from naturality. Put H=THH(B), viewed as a commutative algebra object in cyclotomic spectra. In AMMN Proposition 2.8 and Corollary 2.10 the comparison diagrams are obtained by tensoring the cyclotomic coefficient maps with H. They are thus diagrams of H-modules; the maps are id_H tensor the displayed coefficient maps. The maps from H and the linearization H tensor HZ→HH(B) used in their Theorem 2.12 are H-linear, and so is the subsequent projection to relative HH(B/R). The TC functor is lax symmetric monoidal. Applying it sends these H-module diagrams to diagrams of TC(B)-modules. The circle invariants, orbits, and norm fiber have the corresponding module actions, with the norm projection formula making the fiber action agree with the usual HC^- action on Sigma HC. Every equivalence used to replace a term is consequently an equivalence in the module category and may be inverted there. The henselian relative K-to-TC equivalence in the proof of Theorem A respects the action of K(B), because it is induced by the multiplicative cyclotomic trace. The multiplicativity used here is the E-infinity refinement established by Blumberg–Gepner–Tabuada, Uniqueness of the multiplicative cyclotomic trace, arXiv:1103.3923, Theorem 7.4. The underlying equivalence of module spectra is an equivalence in that category. Restricting the TC(B)-action along the trace proves the asserted K(B)-linearity through its negative cyclic Chern character.

5. The mixed complex and the actual product. In our relative dimension two and residue characteristic five, the required normalized HKR comparison is integral, because 2 is invertible in R. On a normalized Hochschild chain a_0[a_1|...|a_j] for j=0,1,2, send it to
 (1/j!) a_0 da_1 wedge ... wedge da_j,
and send chains in higher degrees to zero. The Hochschild boundary maps to zero. On the Connes circle operator, the alternating cyclic sum gives (j+1) times exterior differentiation before normalization, so the factors 1/j! make this map intertwine the circle operator with d. For j=2 the target of the latter is Omega^3=0. The relative smooth HKR identification of Hochschild homology with Omega^j sends the antisymmetrized form to j! times the unnormalized displayed expression, so this normalized map induces the identity on each homology group. All higher relative HH groups vanish. Thus it is a quasi-isomorphism of mixed complexes. Its completed version has the finite continuous form modules, by the finite-precision comparison in step 2.

Use a bookkeeping variable t of homological degree -2 for the cyclic complexes; a j-form has homological degree j and the mixed differential is t d. The ordinary cyclic model consists of the nonpositive powers 1,t^(-1),t^(-2),... with terms having positive t powers discarded. In homological degree two it has Omega^2 and t^(-1) B, and its differential maps t^(-1)b to db in degree one. Degree-three boundaries in the top-form component are d Omega^1. Hence its homology in degree two decomposes as
 Omega^2[1/5]/d Omega^1[1/5] direct-sum t^(-1)ker(d:B[1/5]→Omega^1[1/5]).
This proves the claimed top-form summand with ordinary image.

For the coefficient ring R relative to itself the only form term is E in degree zero, so its degree-two ordinary cyclic group is t^(-1)E. Let beta correspond to t^(-1)b. Pullback to B gives the same scalar representative; b is constant for relative differentiation over R. The normalized negative cyclic Chern character of a line bundle J is its rank-one term plus t c_1^dR(J); all higher form terms vanish in relative dimension two. Therefore the character of 1-[J] has rank zero and equals -t c_1^dR(J). The negative-cyclic action on ordinary cyclic homology multiplies these two representatives to -b c_1^dR(J). Equivalently, at the bar-complex level the shuffle part gives this wedge product and every cyclic-shuffle correction inserts the relative scalar b into a normalized bar slot and vanishes. This is exactly the module action from step 4, not a separately postulated operation.

It follows that a nonzero displayed top-form component cannot come from a zero rationalized completed relative K-class. Applying the natural map from actual relative K-theory proves the last assertion and its finite-sum version. Nothing in the proof identifies actual K-groups with their spectral completions.

## intuition
The base ramification contributes only bounded cotangent torsion in each finite range; after rationalized completion the relative cyclic fiber remains available, and its module action converts a point K3 coefficient times a line-bundle class into the coefficient times its de Rham Chern class.
