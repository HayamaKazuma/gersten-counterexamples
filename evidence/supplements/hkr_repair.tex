Let $A=B_0$, and let $C_\bullet(A/W)$ denote its normalized
Hochschild mixed complex, with operators $b$ and $B$.
For $0\leq j\leq D$ define
\[
 q_j(a_0[a_1|\cdots|a_j])
   =\frac{D!}{j!}a_0\,da_1\wedge\cdots\wedge da_j,
\]
and put $q_j=0$ for $j>D$. These are integral maps: $D!/j!$ is
an integer. The commutativity of $A$ gives $q_{j-1}b=0$.
The defining formula for Connes' operator gives
\[
 q_{j+1}B=dq_j.
\]
Indeed its $j+1$ cyclic summands have the same image after the
Koszul signs are included, and
$(j+1)D!/(j+1)!=D!/j!$. At $j=D$ both sides are zero since
$\Omega^{D+1}_{A/W}=0$. Thus $q$ is a morphism of mixed
complexes from $C_\bullet(A/W)$ to
$(\Omega^\bullet_{A/W},0,d)$.

The usual Hochschild--Kostant--Rosenberg isomorphism for an
essentially smooth algebra is induced by antisymmetrization.
The composite of antisymmetrization with $q_j$ is multiplication
by $D!$. In particular $q$ induces $D!$ on
$HH_j(A/W)=\Omega^j_{A/W}$ for $0\leq j\leq D$, and both sides
have zero Hochschild homology in higher degrees. Since $A$ is
$W$-flat and $\Omega^j_{A/W}$ is finite projective over $A$,
multiplication by $D!$ is injective on every form module.
The homotopy groups of the cone of $q$ are therefore the
cokernels $\Omega^j_{A/W}/D!$ in degrees $0,\ldots,D$;
there is no additional top kernel in degree $D+1$.

View this cone as an object with circle action. Its equivariant
Postnikov filtration has $D+1$ nonzero layers. Each layer is a
shifted ordinary module with trivial circle action and is killed
by $p^{v_p(D!)}$, because all primes other than $p$ are units in
$W$. The elementary extension argument therefore kills the
entire equivariant cone by $p^{(D+1)v_p(D!)}$. Applying derived
$p$-completion, circle homotopy orbits, homotopy fixed points,
or Tate constructions preserves this scalar null-homotopy.
After rationalization, $q/D!$ consequently gives the required
continuous mixed-HKR comparison.

In the present applications $p=5$ and $D=2$ or $3$.
Then $D!$ is already a unit in $W$, so $q/D!$ is an integral
mixed-complex quasi-isomorphism. Its formula is
$a_0[a_1|\cdots|a_j]\mapsto a_0da_1\wedge\cdots\wedge da_j/j!$.
The shuffle product becomes exterior multiplication. The
cyclic-shuffle term involving a coefficient from the relative
base has zero image, since that coefficient occurs in a
normalized bar slot, or equivalently has zero relative
differential. These facts give the precise product formula
used for the scalar point coefficient and the rank-zero line
character.
